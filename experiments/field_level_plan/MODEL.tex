\section{Notation and the objects that already exist}
\label{sec:notation}

Sky pixels are indexed $p\in\{1,\dots,N_{\rm pix}\}$ in HEALPix RING ordering at
$N_{\rm side}=64$ ($N_{\rm pix}=49\,152$, $\Omega_{\rm pix}=4\pi/N_{\rm pix}$).
Redshift is carried on the package grid $\{z_n\}_{n=1}^{N_{\rm grid}}$
($N_{\rm grid}=1086$).  A \emph{voxel} is $v=(p,z)$.  Coarse radial shells used
by the field fit are indexed $g\in\{1,\dots,G_s\}$ with edges $\{z_g^-,z_g^+\}$.
Catalogs (tracers) are indexed $k\in\{1,\dots,K\}$.

Cosmological and population parameters are $\Lambda$ (containing $H_0$);
survey parameters are
$\theta=(\log_{10}n_0,\,\delta,\,\theta_{\rm sel},\,b_{\rm miss},\dots)$, per
catalog where suffixed.  Throughout, the \emph{expected all-galaxy comoving
density} is
\begin{equation}
  \bar n(z;\Lambda,\theta)\;\equiv\;\frac{dN_{\rm exp}}{dz\,d\Omega}
  \;=\;n_0\,(1+z)^{\delta}\,\frac{dV_c}{dz\,d\Omega}(z;\Lambda),
  \label{eq:dNexp}
\end{equation}
which is the code object \verb|grids.dN_exp|.  $C(z;\theta)\in[0,1]$ is the
completeness curve; under \verb|c_mode="per_pixel"| it is $C_p(z)$ (a per-pixel
KDE ratio), under \verb|"aggregate"| it is the sky-aggregate $\bar C(z)$, and
under \verb|"selection"| it is the parametric $C_{\rm sel}(z;\theta_{\rm sel})$
formed from a truncated luminosity function (optionally stratified, giving a
per-pixel \emph{depth} routing $C_{s(p)}(z;\theta_{\rm sel})$ through a stamped
stratum map --- single-catalog only).  The production 259-event run uses
\verb|c_mode=selection|, no stratification, and \emph{no LSS at all}: it runs
\verb|--use_lss false| with \verb|lss_completion none|
(\texttt{logs/ns\_joint\_sel\_1119811.out:68,\,73,\,232}), so the field-level
path would be the first LSS ever used in production.

\section{The current state as one generative model}
\label{sec:current}

The pieces are usually described as three separate machines (a builder, a
table, a likelihood).  They are one generative model, and writing it as one is
what exposes where it is exact and where it is a plug-in.

\subsection{The galaxy point process and its thinning}

Conditional on a latent log-density contrast field $s(p,z)$, galaxies are an
inhomogeneous Poisson process on $(p,z)$ with intensity
\begin{equation}
  \Lambda_{\rm gal}(p,z)\;=\;\bar n(z;\Lambda,\theta)\,
  \exp\!\Big[b_{\rm gal}\,s(p,z)-\tfrac12 b_{\rm gal}^2\sigma^2(p,z)\Big],
  \qquad \sigma^2(p,z)\equiv\mathrm{Var}\,[s(p,z)],
  \label{eq:galintensity}
\end{equation}
the lognormal density model.  The shift $-\tfrac12b^2\sigma^2$ enforces
$\mathbb E_{\rm prior}\big[e^{b s-\frac12 b^2\sigma^2}\big]=1$, so
\eqref{eq:galintensity} has prior-mean intensity exactly $\bar n$: the field
\emph{redistributes} galaxies and does not create them.  This mean-one
convention is the single most load-bearing convention in the whole stack and
reappears three times below.

The survey observes each galaxy independently with probability
$f_p\,C(z;\theta)$, where $f_p\in[0,1]$ is the per-pixel areal completeness
($f_p=1-\texttt{masked\_frac}_p$; $f_p\equiv1$ in today's code) and $C$ is the
magnitude/flux completeness.  By the Poisson thinning theorem the observed
(\emph{catalogued}) and unobserved (\emph{missing}) galaxies are two
\textbf{independent} Poisson processes,
\begin{align}
  \Lambda_{\rm cat}(p,z) &= f_p\,C(z;\theta)\;\Lambda_{\rm gal}(p,z), \label{eq:thin-cat}\\
  \Lambda_{\rm miss}(p,z) &= \big[1-f_p\,C(z;\theta)\big]\;\Lambda_{\rm gal}(p,z),
  \label{eq:thin-miss}
\end{align}
and, crucially,
\begin{equation}
  p\big(\text{missing galaxies}\,\big|\,\text{catalogued galaxies},\,s\big)
  \;=\;p\big(\text{missing galaxies}\,\big|\,s\big).
  \label{eq:thinning-independence}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:thinning-independence} is exact, and it is the entire
resolution of the double-conditioning question of \S\ref{sec:double}.  It says:
\emph{once the field is fixed, the catalog carries no further information about
what is missing}.  Everything the catalog knows about the missing galaxies, it
knows through $s$.

\subsection{The dark-siren redshift--sky prior as it is coded}

The likelihood does not integrate \eqref{eq:thin-cat} --- it uses the catalog
itself.  For a dark siren with parameters $(z,\hat n_p)$,
\begin{equation}
  p\big(z,p\,\big|\,\Lambda,\theta,Q\big)
  \;=\;\frac{N_{{\rm obs},p}\,\mathcal K_p(z)\;+\;\big[1-C(z;\theta)\big]\,
  \bar n(z;\Lambda,\theta)\;Q_p(z)}{\mathcal Z(\Lambda,\theta,Q)},
  \label{eq:zprior}
\end{equation}
\hfill[code: \texttt{prior.py:668--673}, \texttt{:755--758}]

\noindent where $N_{{\rm obs},p}$ is the integer galaxy count in pixel $p$ and
$\mathcal K_p(z)$ the pixel's photo-$z$ kernel density
(\verb|eval_log_catalog_prior_state|).  The normaliser $\mathcal Z$ is either
per-pixel ($\mathcal Z_p$, ``conditional'' convention) or survey-global
(``field'' convention, \texttt{completion.py:1780--1810}).  The consumed
missing density is
\begin{equation}
  \frac{dN_{\rm miss}}{dz\,d\Omega}(p,z)=\big[1-C(z;\theta)\big]\,
  \bar n(z;\Lambda,\theta)\;Q_p(z),\qquad
  Q_p(z)\equiv e^{\mathrm{clip}(\log Q_p(z),\,\pm7)},
  \label{eq:dNmiss}
\end{equation}
relaxed to $dN_{\rm miss}=dN_{\rm exp}$ (i.e. $C:=0$, $Q:=1$) above the survey
depth $z_{\rm depth}$.\hfill[code: \texttt{completion.py:1280,\,1295}]

Comparing \eqref{eq:dNmiss} with \eqref{eq:thin-miss} identifies
\begin{equation}
  Q_p(z)\;\equiv\;\exp\!\Big[b_{\rm miss}\,s(p,z)-\tfrac12 b_{\rm miss}^2\sigma^2(p,z)\Big]
  \quad\text{with } f_p\equiv 1 .
  \label{eq:Qdef}
\end{equation}
So $Q$ \emph{is} the exponentiated latent field, mean-one normalised, and
nothing else.  Its only defect as written is that in the current code it is a
\textbf{fixed table read from disk} rather than a function of the sampled
parameters.

\subsection{The offline field model: Poisson-lognormal on a low-rank basis}

Let $\{Z_j\}_{j=1}^{M}$ be inducing nodes on $\mathbb S^2\times\zeta$ with
$\zeta=\log(1+z)$ (a Fibonacci sphere of $M_{\rm sph}$ points crossed with
$M_z$ nodes uniform in $\zeta$, $M=M_{\rm sph}M_z$), and $k(\cdot,\cdot)$ the
separable kernel $k=k_{\rm sph}\otimes k_{\zeta}$ with amplitude $a$ and
lengthscales $(\ell_{\rm ang},\ell_\zeta)$.  With
$L L^{\!\top}=K_{ZZ}+j\mathbb 1$ define the Nystr\"om design
\begin{equation}
  \Phi \;=\; K_{XZ}\,L^{-\!\top}\ \in\ \mathbb R^{V\times M},
  \qquad
  s \;=\; \Phi\,\xi,\qquad \xi\sim\mathcal N(0,\mathbb 1_M),
  \label{eq:lowrank}
\end{equation}
so that $\mathrm{Cov}(s)=\Phi\Phi^{\!\top}=K_{XZ}(K_{ZZ}+j\mathbb 1)^{-1}K_{ZX}$
and the per-voxel prior variance is
$\sigma^2_{\rm prior}(v)=\sum_i\Phi_{vi}^2$.  $\Phi$ is never materialised: it
factorises exactly as $\Phi=\Phi_{\rm sph}\otimes\Phi_\zeta$ (a dense $\Phi$ at
$M=3780$ would be $47.6$\,GB).

The fit is over solve voxels $v=(p,g)$ carrying integer counts $N_v$ and a base
\begin{equation}
  \bar\lambda_v\;=\;\int_{z_g^-}^{z_g^+}\! C(z;\theta_{\rm ref})\,
  \bar n(z;\Lambda_{\rm ref},\theta_{\rm ref})\,dz ,
  \label{eq:base}
\end{equation}
which is $p$-independent under \verb|c_mode| $\in$
\{\texttt{aggregate},\texttt{selection}\} and $p$-dependent under
\verb|per_pixel|.  The MAP problem is
\begin{equation}
  J(\xi)=\tfrac12\|\xi\|^2+\sum_v\big[\lambda_v-N_v\log\lambda_v\big],
  \qquad
  \lambda_v=\bar\lambda_v\exp\!\Big[b\,(\Phi\xi)_v-\tfrac12 b^2\sigma^2_{\rm prior}(v)\Big],
  \label{eq:Jpoisson}
\end{equation}
\hfill[code: \texttt{lognormal\_completion.py:660--700}]
\begin{equation}
  \nabla J=\xi+b\,\Phi^{\!\top}(\lambda-N),
  \qquad
  H=\nabla^2 J=\mathbb 1+b^2\,\Phi^{\!\top}\mathrm{diag}(\lambda)\,\Phi .
  \label{eq:poissonHess}
\end{equation}
$J$ is convex; $H\succeq\mathbb 1$ always, so the problem is well posed even
when $V<M$.  The field is clipped at $|s|\le10$ with a KKT projection so that
objective, gradient and Hessian describe the same clipped function.

The Laplace posterior is $p(\xi\,|\,\{N\})\approx\mathcal N(\hat\xi,H^{-1})$,
sampled as
\begin{equation}
  \xi_m=\hat\xi+L_H^{-\!\top}g_m,\qquad g_m\sim\mathcal N(0,\mathbb 1_M),
  \qquad L_HL_H^{\!\top}=H .
  \label{eq:laplacedraws}
\end{equation}

\subsection{The two $Q$ evaluations, and the variance shift}

Let $\phi_u\in\mathbb R^M$ be the Nystr\"om row at an \emph{output} voxel
$u=(p,z)$ on the fine grid, and
\begin{equation}
  \sigma^2_{\rm prior}(u)=\|\phi_u\|^2,
  \qquad
  \sigma^2_{\rm post}(u)=\phi_u^{\!\top}H^{-1}\phi_u=\big\|L_H^{-1}\phi_u\big\|^2 .
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[posterior-mean $Q$ and its variance shift]
\label{lem:varshift}
Under the Laplace posterior \eqref{eq:laplacedraws},
\begin{equation}
  \log\mathbb E\big[Q_u\big]
  \;=\;b\,\phi_u^{\!\top}\hat\xi
  \;-\;\tfrac12 b^2\Big[\sigma^2_{\rm prior}(u)-\sigma^2_{\rm post}(u)\Big].
  \label{eq:postmeanQ}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$Q_u=\exp\!\big[b\,\phi_u^{\!\top}\xi-\tfrac12b^2\sigma^2_{\rm prior}(u)\big]$
by \eqref{eq:Qdef}--\eqref{eq:lowrank}.  For $\xi\sim\mathcal N(\hat\xi,H^{-1})$
and any $a$, $\mathbb E[e^{a^{\!\top}\xi}]=\exp(a^{\!\top}\hat\xi+\tfrac12
a^{\!\top}H^{-1}a)$.  Put $a=b\,\phi_u$.
\end{proof}

\noindent Equation~\eqref{eq:postmeanQ} \emph{is} the table the GW likelihood
consumes today [code: \texttt{lognormal\_completion.py:967}], and the per-draw
member tables are
\begin{equation}
  \log Q^{(m)}_u=b\,\phi_u^{\!\top}\xi_m-\tfrac12 b^2\sigma^2_{\rm prior}(u)
  \label{eq:memberQ}
\end{equation}
\hfill[code: \texttt{lognormal\_completion.py:973}]

\noindent so that $\tfrac1M\sum_m Q^{(m)}_u\to\mathbb E[Q_u]$ exactly, by
Lemma~\ref{lem:varshift}.  Data-free voxels have
$\sigma^2_{\rm post}=\sigma^2_{\rm prior}$ and $\phi_u^{\!\top}\hat\xi\to0$, so
$Q\to1$: ``no information, no modulation.''

\begin{remark}[the plug-in gap, stated exactly]
\label{rem:plugin}
The GW likelihood is a nonlinear functional of $Q$ --- $Q$ appears inside
$\log$ in \eqref{eq:zprior} and inside $\log\mu$ in the selection term --- so
\begin{equation}
  \log\mathbb E_\xi\big[\mathcal L\big(Q(\xi)\big)\big]\;\neq\;
  \log\mathcal L\big(\mathbb E_\xi[Q]\big).
\end{equation}
Running the deterministic table is a \emph{plug-in}, exact only to first order
in the posterior spread of $s$.  The member ensemble is the correction; its own
error budget is Proposition~\ref{prop:jensen}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The per-$z$ mean-one budget constraint as a gauge condition}

\begin{proposition}[$Q$ has no monopole freedom]
\label{prop:gauge}
For any positive function $\alpha(z)$, the map
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal G_\alpha:\quad
  Q_p(z)\;\longmapsto\;\alpha(z)\,Q_p(z),
  \qquad
  \big[1-C(z;\theta)\big]\,\bar n(z;\Lambda,\theta)
  \;\longmapsto\;
  \frac{\big[1-C(z;\theta)\big]\,\bar n(z;\Lambda,\theta)}{\alpha(z)}
\end{equation}
leaves $dN_{\rm miss}$ in \eqref{eq:dNmiss}, and therefore the entire GW
likelihood, exactly invariant.  Hence the per-$z$ monopole of $Q$ is not
identified by the GW data; it is identified only by whatever fixes
$(n_0,\delta,\theta_{\rm sel})$.
\end{proposition}

The code fixes this gauge, at build time, by the condition
\begin{equation}
  \log Q_p(z)\;\longleftarrow\;\log Q_p(z)
  -\log\!\left[\frac{\sum_{p\in\mathcal F}w_p(z)\,Q_p(z)}{\sum_{p\in\mathcal F}w_p(z)}\right],
  \qquad
  w_p(z)=\big[1-C_p(z)\big]\,\bar n(z),
  \label{eq:renorm}
\end{equation}
applied over the \emph{fitted footprint} $\mathcal F$, to the MAP table and to
\textbf{each member independently}.\hfill[code:
\texttt{lognormal\_completion.py:502--560};
\texttt{build\_lognormal\_completion.py:582,\,990}]

\begin{proposition}[when the gauge fixing is exactly what the likelihood consumes]
\label{prop:budgetexact}
Let $\mathcal B(z)=\sum_{p=1}^{N_{\rm pix}}\big[1-C_p(z;\theta)\big]\bar n(z;\theta)Q_p(z)$
be the budget the likelihood actually integrates, and
$\mathcal B_0(z)$ the same sum with $Q\equiv1$.  Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Under \verb|c_mode| $\in\{\texttt{aggregate},\texttt{selection}\}$ in the
\texttt{gp3d} builder, $\mathcal F$ is the \emph{whole sky}
(\verb|fit = np.arange(n_pix)|) and $w_p(z)=w(z)$ is $p$-independent
(\verb|np.tile|).  Then \eqref{eq:renorm} reduces to
$\langle Q\rangle_{\rm sky}=1$, and
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal B(z)=\big[1-C(z;\theta)\big]\bar n(z;\theta)\sum_p Q_p(z)
  =\big[1-C(z;\theta)\big]\bar n(z;\theta)\,N_{\rm pix}=\mathcal B_0(z)
\end{equation}
\textbf{exactly, for every $z$, every member, and every $\theta$} --- the
gauge condition is $\theta$-free because the weights are.
\item Under \verb|c_mode="per_pixel"|, $\mathcal F$ is the occupied subset and
$w_p$ depends on $p$ and on $\theta$.  Then $\mathcal B(z)\neq\mathcal B_0(z)$
in general, by two separate mechanisms: (i) the off-footprint pixels
($Q\equiv1$) are outside the constraint but inside the consumed sum, and (ii)
the weights used at build time are evaluated at $\theta_{\rm ref}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}
Proposition~\ref{prop:budgetexact}(1) is a stronger statement than the plan's
\S4.2 assumes.  In the \emph{production} configuration
(\verb|c_mode=selection|) the mean-one identity is already exactly the consumed
identity; the discrepancy the reviews flag is real, but confined to
\verb|per_pixel|.  This matters for the proposed model because it says the
budget/placement split survives a change of $\theta$ \emph{without any frozen
weight operator} whenever the completeness base is sky-uniform.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[the renormalisation does not commute with the ensemble mean]
\label{rem:noncommute}
Write $\Pi$ for the map \eqref{eq:renorm}.  $\Pi$ is nonlinear in $Q$, so
\begin{equation}
  \frac1M\sum_m \Pi\big[Q^{(m)}\big]\;\neq\;\Pi\big[\mathbb E[Q]\big] .
\end{equation}
The deterministic table (renormalise the posterior mean) and the member
ensemble (renormalise each draw) therefore target slightly different objects.
The bias is $O\!\big(\mathrm{Var}_{\rm sky}(Q)/N_{\rm pix}\big)$ per shell and
is second order, but it is not zero, and it is the reason the members carry
``placement uncertainty only, zero budget uncertainty'' --- a statement that is
a modelling choice, not a theorem.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Member-ensemble marginalisation}

With members $\{Q^{(m)}\}_{m=1}^{M_{\rm draw}}$ the likelihood is
\begin{equation}
  \log\mathcal L(\Lambda,\theta)
  \;=\;\log\frac1{M_{\rm draw}}\sum_{m}\mathcal L_m(\Lambda,\theta)
  \;=\;\mathop{\mathrm{LSE}}_m\;\log\mathcal L_m\;-\;\log M_{\rm draw},
  \label{eq:membermarg}
\end{equation}
\hfill[code: \texttt{likelihood/core.py:1274}]
\begin{equation}
  \log\mathcal L_m=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\rm ev}}
  \log\!\int\! d\vartheta\;
  \frac{p(d_i|\vartheta)\,\pi_{\rm pop}(\vartheta|\Lambda)\,
        p_z\big(z,p\,|\,\Lambda,\theta,Q^{(m)}\big)}{\pi_{\rm PE}(\vartheta)}
  \;-\;N_{\rm ev}\log\mu\big(\Lambda,\theta,Q^{(m)}\big).
  \label{eq:Lm}
\end{equation}
\hfill[code: \texttt{likelihood/core.py:1249--1262}]

\noindent The member index enters \textbf{both} the per-event (PE) term and the
selection term $\mu$; \verb|core.py:1372--1380| enforces this as an all-or-none
guard, and the per-member $N_{\rm eff}$/variance guard already runs inside the
member \verb|vmap|.  This is the correct structure: a member is one realisation
of the universe, and the same universe must be used to place hosts and to
compute the detected fraction.

\begin{proposition}[Jensen bias of \eqref{eq:membermarg}]
\label{prop:jensen}
\eqref{eq:membermarg} is an unbiased estimator of $\mathcal L$ but a biased
estimator of $\log\mathcal L$.  If $\log\mathcal L_m$ has spread $\sigma$ nats
and is approximately Gaussian across members, then to leading order
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb E\big[\log\widehat{\mathcal L}_{M}\big]-\log\mathcal L
  \;\simeq\;-\,\frac12\frac{\mathrm{Var}\big(\widehat{\mathcal L}_M\big)}{\mathcal L^2}
  \;=\;-\,\frac{e^{\sigma^2}-1}{2M},
  \qquad
  \frac{\mathrm{ESS}}{M}\;\simeq\;e^{-\sigma^2}.
  \label{eq:jensenbias}
\end{equation}
Requiring $|{\rm bias}|<\epsilon$ nats therefore requires
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;M\;>\;\frac{e^{\sigma^2}-1}{2\epsilon}\;}
  \label{eq:Mrequired}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\mathcal L_m$ lognormal with $\mathbb E[\mathcal L_m]=\mathcal L$ gives
$\mathrm{Var}(\mathcal L_m)/\mathcal L^2=e^{\sigma^2}-1$ and hence
$\mathrm{Var}(\widehat{\mathcal L}_M)/\mathcal L^2=(e^{\sigma^2}-1)/M$.
Expand $\log$ about $\mathcal L$ to second order.
\end{proof}

\noindent \eqref{eq:Mrequired} is sharper than the usual ``$M\sim e^{\sigma^2}$''
folklore and it is brutal: at the plan's upper estimate $\sigma\simeq2.6$ nats,
$e^{\sigma^2}=863$, so $\epsilon=0.1$ nat needs $M>4.3\times10^3$, and $M=8$
carries a $54$-nat bias.

\begin{remark}[what actually has to be gated: the frozen-noise surrogate]
\label{rem:crn}
The whitened draws $g_m$ in \eqref{eq:laplacedraws} are generated \emph{once}
and reused at every $\theta$ (common random numbers).  Therefore
$\widehat{\mathcal L}_M(\theta)$ is a \textbf{deterministic, smooth function of
$\theta$}, not a noisy one: the sampler explores an exactly reproducible
surrogate surface.  The bias of Proposition~\ref{prop:jensen} is a fixed
perturbation, and only its \emph{$\theta$-variation}
\begin{equation}
  \Delta(\theta)\;=\;\log\widehat{\mathcal L}_M(\theta)-\log\mathcal L(\theta),
  \qquad
  \mathop{\mathrm{osc}}_\theta\,\Delta\;\equiv\;
  \max_\theta\Delta-\min_\theta\Delta
  \label{eq:osc}
\end{equation}
distorts the posterior; a constant offset is absorbed into the evidence.  The
consequence for validation is unforgiving: every determinism diagnostic
(repeat-to-repeat identity, adjacent-$\theta$ smoothness) passes
\emph{by construction} under CRN, and passes just as well on a badly distorted
surrogate.  Only \eqref{eq:osc}, measured against a large-$M$ reference,
discriminates.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The $K\ge2$ shared-latent joint solve}

For $K$ catalogs sharing one field, the stacked design is
$\Phi'_k=b_k\Phi_k$ with $b=1$ passed to the solver, so that the solver's
default $\sigma^2_{\rm vox}=\sum_i(\Phi'_k)^2_{vi}=b_k^2\sum_i(\Phi_k)^2_{vi}$
is the per-survey-correct mean-one shift automatically.  Then
\begin{align}
  J(\xi)&=\tfrac12\|\xi\|^2+\sum_{k=1}^K\sum_{v\in\mathcal V_k}
  \big[\lambda_{kv}-N_{kv}\log\lambda_{kv}\big],
  \qquad
  \lambda_{kv}=\bar\lambda_{kv}\,
  e^{\,b_k(\Phi_k\xi)_v-\frac12 b_k^2\sigma^2_{kv}},
  \label{eq:jointJ}\\
  H&=\mathbb 1+\sum_{k=1}^K b_k^2\,\Phi_k^{\!\top}\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_k)\,\Phi_k ,
  \label{eq:jointH}
\end{align}
one Laplace ensemble $\{\xi_m\}$, and per-survey tables
$\log Q_k^{(m)}=b_k\phi^{\!\top}\xi_m-\tfrac12 b_k^2\sigma^2_{\rm prior}$.
Member $m$ of every catalog is then the same physical realisation --- a theorem,
not a check --- and it is stamped as a shared \verb|realization_set_id| which
the inference verifies.

\begin{remark}[three verified limitations of this path, as of \texttt{0c5b3db}]
\label{rem:jointgaps}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{It is \texttt{per\_pixel}-only.}  The joint builder calls the
shared assembly without a \verb|c_mode| argument,
\begin{verbatim}
_assemble_gp3d_survey(path, cosmo=cosmo, survey=survey_k, z_s=z_s, edges_s=edges_s)
                                       [build_joint_lognormal_completion.py:180-181]
\end{verbatim}
so it silently takes the default \verb|c_mode="per_pixel"| and the
occupied-only base.  Neither \texttt{aggregate} nor \texttt{selection} is
reachable at $K\ge2$.
\item \textbf{It never fixes the budget gauge.}  \verb|renormalize_q_mean_one|
is called by the radial builder (\texttt{:582}) and the $K=1$ \texttt{gp3d}
builder (\texttt{:990}) and by \emph{nothing} in
\verb|build_joint_lognormal_completion.py|.  The $K\ge2$ tables therefore carry
the full Jensen monopole of Lemma~\ref{lem:varshift} --- the effect measured at
$+55\%$ budget inflation --- which the $K=1$ tables have removed.
\item \textbf{Its rank cannot resolve any physical scale.}  The joint inducing
grid is hard-coded at $M_{\rm sph}=32$, $M_z=6$, $z_{\rm node,hi}=3$
($M=192$), giving a $\zeta$ node spacing $\log(1+3)/5=0.277$.  The \emph{hard}
radial resolution guard demands $\ell_\zeta\ge0.277$, i.e.
$L_{\rm smooth}\ge0.277\,(1+z_{\rm ref})\,d\chi/dz\simeq1.34\,$Gpc at
$z_{\rm ref}\simeq0.24$.  Any correlation length a DESI-like survey can
actually support ($50$--$190$\,Mpc, $\ell_\zeta=0.010$--$0.039$) hard-fails the
guard.
\end{enumerate}
Together these say the shared-latent $K\ge2$ path is not merely un-plumbed for
selection completeness: \emph{it cannot today produce a resolved, gauge-fixed,
selection-based shared field at all}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Which half of the \texttt{selection}\,$\times\,K\!\ge\!2$ seam is open}
\label{sec:seam}

This is worth stating precisely, because the two halves have moved apart.

\paragraph{The inference half is OPEN.}  At \texttt{master} \texttt{0c5b3db},
$K\ge2$ mixtures under \verb|c_mode="selection"| assemble and evaluate
end-to-end: per-catalog magnitude fits with per-catalog $\theta$ routing,
homogeneous Schechter mixtures with one shared \verb|M_faint_offset|, a
per-catalog $Q$-table $\theta$ firewall (cosmology checked globally, survey
parameters checked against \emph{catalog $k$'s own} table), and a two-catalog
likelihood fixture pinning the identical-catalogs-collapse-to-$K{=}1$ identity
and the no-cross-routing swap invariance
(\texttt{tests/test\_multitracer\_selection.py}).  There is exactly one refusal
left on this side: \emph{stratified} selection is single-catalog only, because
the full-sky stratum map is attached to the shared data bundle while a $K\ge2$
mixture builds each catalog's views from its own bundle, so the map never
reaches catalog $k$'s \verb|EMCatalog| and its per-stratum empty-pixel budget
would be silently missing (\texttt{inference/parameters.py:460--470}).  That is
a data-plumbing limitation, not a modelling one.

\paragraph{The builder half is CLOSED.}  Remark~\ref{rem:jointgaps} is the
statement.  Operationally: the joint builder is the \emph{only} producer of a
shared \verb|realization_set_id|
(\verb|save_lss_completion_hdf5| mints a fresh \verb|uuid4| whenever one is not
supplied, so $K$ independently built single-catalog tables carry $K$ different
ids), and $K\ge2$ \verb|--lss_marginalize| requires equal, non-null ids unless
\verb|--allow_unverified_shared_lss_members| is passed --- which downgrades the
matched shared-field prior to an independent-fields product prior, i.e. throws
away exactly the cross-tracer coupling the multitracer analysis exists to
exploit.  So a $K\ge2$ shared-latent LSS marginalisation under a
\texttt{selection} (or even \texttt{aggregate}) completeness is not refused; it
is \textbf{not constructible}.

\paragraph{Consequence for the proposal.}  The seam does not need to be closed
by extending the joint builder --- passing \verb|c_mode| through
\texttt{build\_joint\_lognormal\_completion.py:180}, calling
\verb|renormalize_q_mean_one|, and unfreezing $(M_{\rm sph},M_z,z_{\rm node,hi})$
would close it, and that is a genuine short-term option.  But the field-level
hierarchy of \S\ref{sec:proposed} closes it \emph{by construction}: one $\xi$
shared across $K$ tracers makes ``member $m$ of every catalog is the same
realisation'' a theorem, retires \verb|realization_set_id| and the whole
provenance firewall, and removes the builder's frozen rank, frozen $\theta$ and
frozen completeness mode simultaneously.  That is the strongest available
argument that the field belongs inside the HBI rather than in an exported
table.

\section{The proposed model}
\label{sec:proposed}

\subsection{The hierarchy}

Promote $\xi$ from a table to a latent variable of the inference:
\begin{multline}
  p\big(H_0,\Lambda,\xi,\{\theta_k\},\{b_k\},b_{\rm GW},\{f_k\}\;\big|\;
     d_{\rm GW},\{d_{{\rm gal},k}\}\big)
  \;\propto\;
  \pi(H_0,\Lambda)\,\pi(\{\theta_k\})\,\pi(\{b_k\})\,\pi(b_{\rm GW})\,\pi(\{f_k\})\\
  \times\;\underbrace{\mathcal N\big(\xi;0,\mathbb 1_M\big)}_{\rm (F1)}\;
  \times\;\prod_{k=1}^{K}\underbrace{p\big(\{m_i\}_k\,\big|\,\{z_i\}_k,\theta_k\big)}_{\rm (F2)\ magnitudes}\;
  \times\;\prod_{k=1}^{K}\underbrace{p\big(\{N_{kv}\}\,\big|\,\xi,\theta_k,b_k,f_k,\Lambda\big)}_{\rm (F3)\ counts}\\
  \times\;\underbrace{p\big(d_{\rm GW}\,\big|\,\{d_{{\rm gal},k}\},\xi,b_{\rm GW},\Lambda,\{\theta_k\}\big)}_{\rm (F4)\ GW}
  \label{eq:hierarchy}
\end{multline}
\hfill[new]

\noindent with $f_k=\{f_{k,p}\}_{p}$ the per-pixel areal completeness field of
catalog $k$ (data with a mask-model uncertainty, not a free function).  The
single structural change from \S\ref{sec:current} is that
$\xi$ now appears in (F1), (F3) \emph{and} (F4), so every cosmological proposal
changes the galaxy-field likelihood consistently.  Factor (F2) is the existing
magnitude channel; the chain rule
$p(\{z\},\{p\},\{m\})=p(\{z\})\,p(\{p\}|\{z\})\,p(\{m\}|\{z\},\{p\})$ is what
licenses writing (F2) and (F3) side by side.

\subsection{(F3): the count likelihood}

On solve voxels $v=(p,g)$:
\begin{equation}
  N_{kv}\;\sim\;\mathrm{Poisson}\Big[
  \underbrace{f_{k,p}\,C_k(v;\theta_k)\,\bar N_k(v;\Lambda,\theta_k)}_{\textstyle \bar\lambda_{kv}(\theta_k,\Lambda)}
  \;\exp\!\Big(b_k s_v-\tfrac12 b_k^2\sigma_v^2\Big)\Big],
  \label{eq:countlike}
\end{equation}
\hfill[new]
\begin{equation}
  \bar N_k(v)=\Omega_{\rm pix}\!\!\int_{z_g^-}^{z_g^+}\!\!\bar n(z;\Lambda,\theta_k)\,dz .
\end{equation}
Two consequences must be stated explicitly because they are $C$-side changes:
\begin{itemize}
\item $f_{k,p}$ appears in \eqref{eq:countlike} \emph{and} in the consumed
missing budget, $dN_{\rm miss}=\big[1-f_pC(z;\theta)\big]\bar n\,Q$.  Putting it
on one side only inverts the sign of the inferred incompleteness: a pixel with
$63\%$ areal masking is currently told $63\%$ of its galaxies are a
\emph{density deficit} rather than a \emph{selection deficit}, and that deficit
then re-enters the GW likelihood as a claim about where hosts are not.
\item Under \verb|c_mode="per_pixel"| the base $\bar\lambda_{kv}$ is itself a
plug-in estimate of the same counts $N_{kv}$; \eqref{eq:countlike} is then
circular and must be refused.  \verb|aggregate| and \verb|selection| bases are
functions of $\theta$, not of the counts, and are admissible.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{(F4): the GW-host intensity on the same field}

The GW host intensity is built from the \emph{same} $s$ with its own bias:
\begin{equation}
  \Lambda_{\rm host}(p,z;\Lambda,\theta,\xi)
  \;=\;\psi(z;\Lambda)\;\bar n(z;\Lambda,\theta)\;
  \exp\!\Big[b_{\rm GW}\,s(p,z)-\tfrac12 b_{\rm GW}^2\sigma^2(p,z)\Big],
  \label{eq:hostintensity}
\end{equation}
\hfill[new]
with $\psi$ the merger-rate weighting.  Thinning \eqref{eq:hostintensity} by
the same $f_pC$ gives the catalogued/missing split, and the dark-siren
redshift--sky prior becomes
\begin{equation}
  p\big(z,p\,\big|\,\mathcal C,\xi,\Lambda,\theta\big)
  =\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal C}\delta_{p,p_j}\,\mathcal K_j(z)\,w_j(\Lambda)
        \;+\;\big[1-f_pC(z;\theta)\big]\,\psi\,\bar n\;
        e^{\,b_{\rm GW}s(p,z)-\frac12 b_{\rm GW}^2\sigma^2(p,z)}}
       {\mathcal Z(\Lambda,\theta,\xi)} .
  \label{eq:zprior-new}
\end{equation}
\hfill[new]
Structurally this is \eqref{eq:zprior} with $Q_p(z)$ replaced by an explicit
function of $\xi$ and $b_{\rm GW}$ --- which is why the seam in the code is one
gather: $\log Q\to b_{\rm GW}\,\big(\mathrm{row\_fac}[p]\cdot\phi_\zeta[n]\big)-\rho$.

\subsection{Double conditioning: the exact factorisation}
\label{sec:double}

The catalog enters twice --- as the empirical spike train in
\eqref{eq:zprior-new} and as the data of \eqref{eq:countlike}.  This is the
central correctness question of the whole proposal.

\begin{theorem}[no double use]
\label{thm:double}
Let $\mathcal C$ denote the catalogued galaxies and $\mathcal U$ the missing
ones.  Then, exactly,
\begin{equation}
  p\big(\mathcal C,\,d_{\rm GW}\;\big|\;\xi,\Lambda,\theta\big)
  \;=\;
  \underbrace{p\big(\mathcal C\,\big|\,\xi,\theta,\Lambda\big)}_{\rm (F3)}
  \;\times\;
  \underbrace{p\big(d_{\rm GW}\,\big|\,\mathcal C,\xi,\Lambda,\theta\big)}_{\rm (F4)} ,
  \label{eq:chainrule}
\end{equation}
and the second factor may be evaluated with the host prior
\eqref{eq:zprior-new} \textbf{without any double use of the catalog}, provided
the missing-galaxy intensity is written as a function of $\xi$ alone and never
of $\mathcal C$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\eqref{eq:chainrule} is the chain rule and is exact.  For the second claim, the
GW factor requires (i) the catalogued hosts, which are $\mathcal C$ --- a
conditioning event, used as known positions, not re-modelled --- and (ii) the
intensity of $\mathcal U$.  By the thinning independence
\eqref{eq:thinning-independence},
$p(\mathcal U\,|\,\mathcal C,\xi)=p(\mathcal U\,|\,\xi)$, so the intensity of
$\mathcal U$ is $[1-f_pC]\Lambda_{\rm gal}(\xi)$ and involves $\mathcal C$
nowhere.  The catalog's information about $\mathcal U$ has been fully routed
through (F3) into the posterior for $\xi$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[what breaks it]
\label{rem:doublebreak}
Theorem~\ref{thm:double} fails in exactly two ways, and both are live:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Empirical-Bayes plug-in.}  If the missing intensity is evaluated
at a point estimate $\hat\xi(\mathcal C)$ --- which is precisely what the
current $Q$ table is --- then $\mathcal C$ appears inside the GW factor.
Multiplying that by (F3) uses the same counts twice.  Today the code is
\emph{safe} only because it never multiplies by (F3): it is a conditional model
with an empirical-Bayes prior, not a joint one.  Adding the count likelihood
without promoting $\xi$ to a latent is the single most dangerous half-measure
available.
\item \textbf{The monopole, twice.}  Factor the counts exactly by shell totals
$T_{kg}=\sum_pN_{kpg}$:
\begin{equation}
  p\big(\{N_{kpg}\}\,\big|\,\xi,\theta\big)
  \;=\;
  \underbrace{p\big(\{T_{kg}\}\,\big|\,\xi,\theta\big)}_{\text{budget / monopole}}
  \;\times\;
  \underbrace{p\big(\{N_{kpg}\}\,\big|\,\{T_{kg}\},\xi,\theta\big)}_{\text{placement}} .
  \label{eq:shellfactor}
\end{equation}
The calibration that currently pins $(\log_{10}n_0,\delta)$ is a fit to the
\emph{same} counts over $z\in[0.05,0.3]$, delivered to the run as
\verb|--fixed_parameter_values|.  That prior is a surrogate for the first
factor of \eqref{eq:shellfactor}.  Keeping both the calibration prior
\emph{and} the unconditional Poisson count likelihood squares the monopole
information.  The admissible combinations are exactly two:
\emph{(a)} conditional (multinomial) count likelihood $+$ external calibration
prior, or \emph{(b)} unconditional Poisson count likelihood $+$ a genuinely
independent prior on $(n_0,\delta)$.  Combination (a) is recommended, and it is
what \S\ref{sec:cancel} makes exact.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

\subsection{The exact $\theta$-cancellation criterion for the conditional channel}
\label{sec:cancel}

Write the shell-integrated intensity
\begin{equation}
  \Lambda_{pg}(\theta,\xi)=\int_{z_g^-}^{z_g^+}\!
  f_p\,C(z;\theta)\,\bar n(z;\Lambda,\theta)\;
  e^{\,b\,s_p(z)-\frac12 b^2\sigma_p^2(z)}\,dz ,
  \qquad
  \pi_{pg}=\frac{\Lambda_{pg}}{\sum_{p'}\Lambda_{p'g}} .
  \label{eq:pipg}
\end{equation}
Define the within-shell normalised profile
\begin{equation}
  \omega_g(z;\theta)=\frac{C(z;\theta)\,\bar n(z;\Lambda,\theta)}
  {\int_{g} C\,\bar n\,dz},\qquad \int_g\omega_g\,dz=1,
\end{equation}
so that $\Lambda_{pg}=a_g(\theta,\Lambda)\,f_p\!\int_g\omega_g(z;\theta)\,
e^{b s_p(z)-\frac12 b^2\sigma_p^2(z)}dz$ with $a_g$ $p$-independent.

\begin{proposition}[exact cancellation]
\label{prop:cancel}
$\pi_{pg}$ is exactly independent of $\theta$ (and of $\Lambda$, and of $n_0$,
$\delta$, $\theta_{\rm sel}$, $H_0$, $\Omega_{m}$, $w_0$, $w_a$) if and only if
the base is $p$-separable within the shell --- $\bar\lambda_{p}(z)=u_p\,a(z)$,
true whenever $C$ is sky-uniform --- \textbf{and} the field is shell-constant,
$s_p(z)=s_{pg}$ for $z\in g$.  Under those two conditions
\begin{equation}
  \pi_{pg}=\frac{f_p\,e^{\,b s_{pg}-\frac12 b^2\sigma_{pg}^2}}
                {\sum_{p'}f_{p'}\,e^{\,b s_{p'g}-\frac12 b^2\sigma_{p'g}^2}} .
  \label{eq:pi-exact}
\end{equation}
Otherwise the residual $\theta$-dependence is exactly a within-shell covariance,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial\log\pi_{pg}}{\partial\theta}
  =\frac{\displaystyle\int_g \partial_\theta\omega_g\,e^{b s_p}dz}
        {\displaystyle\int_g \omega_g\,e^{b s_p}dz}
  \;-\;\sum_{p'}\pi_{p'g}\,
  \frac{\displaystyle\int_g \partial_\theta\omega_g\,e^{b s_{p'}}dz}
       {\displaystyle\int_g \omega_g\,e^{b s_{p'}}dz},
  \label{eq:cancelresidual}
\end{equation}
which vanishes identically when $e^{bs_p(z)}$ is constant across the shell.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Immediate from \eqref{eq:pipg}: any $p$-independent factor divides out of the
ratio, and shell-constancy of $s_p$ pulls the exponential out of the
$z$-integral so that the remaining $\int_g\omega_g\,dz=1$ removes all
$\theta$-dependence.  \eqref{eq:cancelresidual} is
$\partial_\theta\log\Lambda_{pg}-\sum_{p'}\pi_{p'g}\partial_\theta\log\Lambda_{p'g}$
with $a_g$ cancelled.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Proposition~\ref{prop:cancel} converts the plan's ``freeze the shell-response
operator $W$'' from a modelling choice into a measurable residual: freezing $W$
at $\theta_{\rm ref}$ is exactly the approximation
$\partial_\theta\omega_g\to0$, and \eqref{eq:cancelresidual} is the quantity to
bound.  It also identifies the correct shell width: shells narrow enough that
$s$ is constant across them make the cancellation exact, but the radial
resolution is bounded below by the photo-$z$ scatter
($\sigma_z\simeq0.023$, $\sigma_\chi\simeq89$\,Mpc), so the honest statement is
that the cancellation is exact only up to the photo-$z$-smoothed radial
structure --- which is precisely the structure the forward convolution puts
into $W$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The per-proposal conditional solve and its warm start}
\label{sec:solve}

For each proposal $(\Lambda,\theta,\{b_k\},b_{\rm GW})$ define
\begin{equation}
  J_\theta(\xi)=\tfrac12\|\xi\|^2-\log p\big(\{N\}\,\big|\,\xi,\theta\big),
\end{equation}
with, in the Poisson form, $\nabla J_\theta$ and $H(\theta)$ as in
\eqref{eq:poissonHess}, and in the conditional multinomial form
\begin{align}
  \nabla J_\theta &= \xi-b\sum_g\Phi_g^{\!\top}\big(N_g-T_g\,\pi_g\big),
  \label{eq:multgrad}\\
  H(\theta) &= \mathbb 1+b^2\sum_g T_g\Big[\Phi_g^{\!\top}\mathrm{diag}(\pi_g)\Phi_g
              -u_gu_g^{\!\top}\Big],\qquad u_g=\Phi_g^{\!\top}\pi_g .
  \label{eq:multhess}
\end{align}
\hfill[new]

\begin{proof}[Derivation of \eqref{eq:multhess}]
With $\eta_p=b\,s_p$ and $\pi_p=e^{\eta_p}/\sum_{p'}e^{\eta_{p'}}$, the
multinomial log-likelihood $\ell=\sum_pN_p\log\pi_p$ has
$\partial\ell/\partial\eta_p=N_p-T\pi_p$ and
$\partial^2\ell/\partial\eta_p\partial\eta_q=-T(\delta_{pq}\pi_p-\pi_p\pi_q)$.
Chain through $\eta=b\Phi\xi$ and negate.
\end{proof}

The rank-one subtraction $-T_gu_gu_g^{\!\top}$ is not optional: it is a
positive-semidefinite term, so omitting it makes $H$ too large, $H^{-1}$ too
small, and the Laplace draws \emph{under}-dispersed --- exactly the direction
that hides the error of Proposition~\ref{prop:jensen}.  It is separable and
therefore free: with $\Phi_g=\Phi_{\rm sph}\otimes\phi_\zeta[g]$,
\begin{equation}
  u_g=v_g\otimes\phi_\zeta[g],\quad v_g=\Phi_{\rm sph}^{\!\top}\pi_g,
  \qquad
  T_g\,u_gu_g^{\!\top}=T_g\,\big(v_gv_g^{\!\top}\big)\otimes
  \big(\phi_\zeta[g]\phi_\zeta[g]^{\!\top}\big).
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[warm starts are admissible under a gradient stopping rule]
\label{prop:warmstart}
Let $\xi_\star$ be the iterate at which the solve stops, with
$\varepsilon=\|\nabla J_\theta(\xi_\star)\|_2$.  Since $H\succeq\mathbb 1$
(the whitened prior),
\begin{equation}
  0\;\le\;J_\theta(\xi_\star)-J_\theta(\hat\xi_\theta)
  \;\simeq\;\tfrac12\,\nabla J^{\!\top}H^{-1}\nabla J
  \;\le\;\frac{\varepsilon^2}{2\,\lambda_{\min}(H)}
  \;\le\;\frac{\varepsilon^2}{2}.
  \label{eq:warmbound}
\end{equation}
Hence \emph{any} start --- including a warm start from a neighbouring proposal
--- yields a likelihood that is history-independent to within
$\varepsilon^2/2$ nats.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}
The blanket prohibition on warm starts is too strong and the blanket permission
is too weak.  \eqref{eq:warmbound} gives the operational rule: stop on
$\|\nabla J\|_2<\varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon^2/2$ at least an order of
magnitude below the $\theta$-oscillation budget of \eqref{eq:osc}.  For a
$0.01$-nat budget, $\varepsilon<0.14$.  What is genuinely inadmissible is a
\emph{fixed iteration count} started from a history-dependent point, because
then the residual is unbounded and $\log\mathcal L$ becomes a functional of the
sampler's traversal order --- at which point nested sampling's
$\mathbb E[\log X_i]=-i/n$ shrinkage argument no longer refers to any
well-defined likelihood.  A deterministic anchored start
$\xi^{(0)}=\hat\xi(\theta_{\rm ref})$ satisfies both requirements trivially and
costs one or two extra Newton steps.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Laplace marginalisation of the field}

\begin{proposition}[the galaxy-side evidence]
\label{prop:laplace}
With the whitened prior $\mathcal N(\xi;0,\mathbb 1_M)$,
\begin{equation}
  \log\mathcal L_{\rm gal}(\theta)
  \;=\;\log\!\int\!d\xi\;\mathcal N(\xi;0,\mathbb 1_M)\;p\big(\{N\}|\xi,\theta\big)
  \;\simeq\;-\,J_\theta(\hat\xi_\theta)\;-\;\tfrac12\log\det H(\theta),
  \label{eq:laplace}
\end{equation}
with \textbf{no} residual $(2\pi)^{\pm M/2}$: the prior normalisation and the
Gaussian integral cancel exactly.  Numerically
$\log\det H=2\sum_i\log (L_H)_{ii}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\int(2\pi)^{-M/2}e^{-J(\xi)}d\xi\simeq(2\pi)^{-M/2}e^{-J(\hat\xi)}(2\pi)^{M/2}|H|^{-1/2}$.
\end{proof}

The $\theta$-gradient, for gradient-based samplers, is
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\log\mathcal L_{\rm gal}
  =-\left.\frac{\partial J_\theta}{\partial\theta}\right|_{\hat\xi_\theta}
  \;-\;\tfrac12\mathrm{tr}\!\left(H^{-1}\frac{dH}{d\theta}\right),
  \qquad
  \frac{dH}{d\theta}=\frac{\partial H}{\partial\theta}
  +\sum_i\frac{\partial H}{\partial\xi_i}\frac{\partial\hat\xi_i}{\partial\theta},
  \label{eq:laplacegrad}
\end{equation}
where the envelope theorem removes $\partial\hat\xi/\partial\theta$ from the
first term (because $\nabla_\xi J=0$ at $\hat\xi$) but \emph{not} from the
second, and the implicit function theorem gives
$\partial\hat\xi/\partial\theta=-H^{-1}\,\partial_\theta\nabla_\xi J$.

\paragraph{Exactness.}  \eqref{eq:laplace} is exact iff $J$ is quadratic in
$\xi$.  The controlling small parameter is the posterior standard deviation of
the linear predictor $s_v$, measured at $\simeq5.6\times10^{-3}$; the leading
Edgeworth correction to the log-evidence is $O(10^{-3})$ nats.  This is
\emph{not} the estimator error of Proposition~\ref{prop:jensen}, which is
larger by orders of magnitude and is a separate budget.

\subsection{The fixed-whitened-draw alternative and its ESS law}
\label{sec:ess}

The cheap alternative to a per-proposal solve is to draw $\{g_m\}$ once and
reuse the resulting $\{\xi_m\}$ at every $\theta$ --- exactly what the member
ensemble does today.  Formally this is importance sampling with proposal
$q=\mathcal N(\hat\xi_0,H_0^{-1})$ and target
$p(\xi|\{N\},\theta)\simeq\mathcal N(\hat\xi_\theta,H_\theta^{-1})$:
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\mathcal L}(\theta)=\frac{\sum_m w_m(\theta)\,
  \mathcal L_{\rm GW}(\theta;\xi_m)}{\sum_m w_m(\theta)},
  \qquad
  w_m(\theta)=\frac{\mathcal N(\xi_m;0,\mathbb 1)\,p(\{N\}|\xi_m,\theta)}{q(\xi_m)} .
  \label{eq:isest}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[the ESS law for a fixed draw set]
\label{prop:esslaw}
If $H_\theta\simeq H_0\equiv H$ and only the mode moves, then under $\xi\sim q$
\begin{equation}
  \log w(\theta)=\big(\hat\xi_\theta-\hat\xi_0\big)^{\!\top}H\big(\xi-\hat\xi_0\big)+\mathrm{const},
  \qquad
  \mathrm{Var}\big[\log w\big]
  =\big\|\hat\xi_\theta-\hat\xi_0\big\|_{H}^{2},
\end{equation}
and hence
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;\frac{\mathrm{ESS}(\theta)}{M_{\rm draw}}\;\simeq\;
  \exp\!\Big(-\big\|\hat\xi_\theta-\hat\xi_0\big\|_{H}^{2}\Big)\;}
  \label{eq:esslaw}
\end{equation}
where $\|x\|_H^2=x^{\!\top}Hx$ is measured in units of the posterior standard
deviation, summed over all $M$ modes.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For two Gaussians with common precision $H$, the log ratio is affine in $\xi$
with coefficient $H(\hat\xi_\theta-\hat\xi_0)$; the variance of an affine form
under $q$ is $(\hat\xi_\theta-\hat\xi_0)^{\!\top}H H^{-1}H(\hat\xi_\theta-\hat\xi_0)$.
Then $\mathrm{ESS}/M=1/(1+\mathrm{Var}[w]/\mathbb E[w]^2)=e^{-\mathrm{Var}[\log w]}$
for lognormal $w$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the criterion is on the total, not the per-mode, displacement]
\label{rem:P7}
\eqref{eq:esslaw} is the exact criterion for when the compressed ensemble is
valid, and it is \textbf{much} stricter than a per-mode statement.  A tolerance
written as $\|\Delta\hat\xi\|_H/\sqrt M<\tau$ implies
$\|\Delta\hat\xi\|_H^2<\tau^2M$ and therefore
$\mathrm{ESS}/M\simeq e^{-\tau^2M}$.  At the plan's rank $M=3780$ and its
proposed $\tau=0.1$, this is $e^{-37.8}\approx4\times10^{-17}$ --- a complete
collapse, from a pin that reads as comfortably tight.  To keep
$\mathrm{ESS}/M>0.1$ one needs
\begin{equation}
  \big\|\hat\xi_\theta-\hat\xi_0\big\|_H^2<\log 10\simeq2.3
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \tau<\sqrt{2.3/M}\;=\;0.025\ \text{at }M=3780 .
  \label{eq:P7corrected}
\end{equation}
The same measured number $\|\hat\xi_\theta-\hat\xi_0\|_H$ therefore plays two
distinct roles and needs two distinct tolerances:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(a)] as a \emph{model-misspecification} diagnostic --- how far the frozen
model's field sits from the exact $\theta$-coupled one --- a per-mode
tolerance $\|\Delta\hat\xi\|_H/\sqrt M<0.1$ is a defensible scale, because what
matters there is whether the fitted field looks different;
\item[(b)] as the \emph{estimator-validity} criterion for reusing one draw set
across $\theta$, only the total \eqref{eq:P7corrected} is admissible, because
importance weights compound over all $M$ modes.
\end{itemize}
Any validation of the fixed-draw path must be stated at
\eqref{eq:P7corrected}, not at a per-mode fraction.  This is the quantitative
reason the $\theta$-freezing of the conditional channel
(Proposition~\ref{prop:cancel}) is worth its cost: when the cancellation is
exact there is no importance sampling at all --- it sets
$\hat\xi_\theta\equiv\hat\xi_0$ identically and makes the ESS perfect by
construction rather than by measurement, and only role (a) remains.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[when the compressed ensemble is \emph{exact}]
\label{prop:compressexact}
If $p(\xi\,|\,d_{\rm gal},\theta)=p(\xi\,|\,d_{\rm gal})$ for all $\theta$,
then a single draw set $\{\xi_m\}\sim p(\xi|d_{\rm gal})$ gives, at every
$\theta$, an unbiased estimator of $\mathcal L(\theta)$ with unit importance
weights.  The only error is the Monte-Carlo error of
Proposition~\ref{prop:jensen}; there is no importance-sampling degeneracy at
all.  Conversely, the full $\theta$-coupled solve is \textbf{required} whenever
\begin{equation}
  \big\|\hat\xi_\theta-\hat\xi_0\big\|_H^2
  \;\gtrsim\;\log\!\Big(\frac{M_{\rm draw}}{\mathrm{ESS}_{\min}}\Big).
  \label{eq:requirefullsolve}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\section{The three limits: one hierarchy, three known analyses}
\label{sec:limits}

Write the single-event likelihood as
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal L_i(\Lambda,\theta,\xi)
  =\int\! dz\,d\hat n\;\;p_i\big(d_i\,\big|\,d_L(z;\Lambda),\hat n\big)\;
  \frac{\Lambda_{\rm cat}(\hat n,z)+\Lambda_{\rm miss}(\hat n,z;\xi)}
       {\int\!\big[\Lambda_{\rm cat}+\Lambda_{\rm miss}\big]} .
  \label{eq:master}
\end{equation}
All three established analyses are limits of \eqref{eq:master}, and the field
marginalisation is exactly the operation that interpolates between them.

\subsection{Limit I --- complete catalog, good localisation: the discrete host sum}

$f_pC\to1$ implies $\Lambda_{\rm miss}\to0$ and $\Lambda_{\rm cat}$ is the
empirical point process.  Then
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal L_i(\Lambda)\;\longrightarrow\;
  \sum_{j\in\Omega_{90}^{(i)}} w_j(\Lambda)\;
  p_i\big(d_i\,\big|\,d_L(z_j;\Lambda),\hat n_j\big),
  \label{eq:limit1}
\end{equation}
the classical statistical standard siren.  $\xi$ drops out of
\eqref{eq:master} identically, so
$p(\xi\,|\,d_{\rm gal},d_{\rm GW})=p(\xi\,|\,d_{\rm gal})$ \emph{exactly} and
the marginalisation is trivial.  \textbf{Exact.}

\subsection{Limit II --- incomplete, good localisation: spikes plus a clustered missing field}

$\Lambda_{\rm tot}$ is a sum of catalog spikes and a smooth clustered
component.  Because a well-localised event's support spans only $O(1$--$10)$
correlation cells at the smoothing scale $L_{\rm smooth}$, the likelihood
depends on $\xi$ essentially through the \emph{point value}
$s(\hat n_i,z)$ in the localisation volume.  Expanding to second order in the
field,
\begin{equation}
  \log\mathbb E_\xi\big[\mathcal L_i\big]
  \;\simeq\;\log\bar{\mathcal L}_i
  \;+\;b_{\rm GW}\big\langle s\big\rangle_{i}
  \;+\;\tfrac12 b_{\rm GW}^2\Big[
   \mathrm{Var}_\xi\big\langle s\big\rangle_{i}
   -\big\langle\sigma^2\big\rangle_{i}\Big]+O(b^3),
  \label{eq:limit2}
\end{equation}
where $\langle\cdot\rangle_i$ is the average against the event's normalised
localisation kernel restricted to the missing component.  The first-order term
is the placement information; the second-order term is the placement
\emph{uncertainty}, and it is exactly what the member ensemble estimates.
\textbf{Exact given the model; the expansion \eqref{eq:limit2} is $O(b^3)$.}

\subsection{Limit III --- very incomplete, diffuse localisation: a correlation-function statement}

Now $\Lambda_{\rm cat}\to0$ and the localisation spans many correlation cells.
Write $p_i(x)$ for the event's normalised localisation density on
$x=(\hat n,z)$.  Expanding \eqref{eq:master} in $b_{\rm GW}s$ and taking the
field expectation over $\xi\sim\mathcal N(\hat\xi,\Sigma_\xi)$,
\begin{equation}
  \log\mathbb E_\xi\Big[\prod_i\mathcal L_i\Big]
  \;\ni\;
  b_{\rm GW}^2\sum_{i<j}
  \iint p_i(x)\,p_j(y)\;\xi_{\rm mm}\big(|x-y|\big)\,d^3x\,d^3y
  \;+\;b_{\rm GW}b_{\rm gal}\sum_i\!\iint p_i(x)\,\delta_g(y)\,\xi_{\rm mm}\,,
  \label{eq:limit3}
\end{equation}
i.e. \textbf{the field-marginalised dark-siren likelihood depends on the field
only through the two-point function smeared by the events' localisation
kernels}.  Decomposing the localisation kernels in spherical harmonics per
tomographic shell, $p_i(\hat n,z)=\sum_{\ell m}p^i_{\ell m}(z)Y_{\ell m}(\hat n)$,
\eqref{eq:limit3} becomes
\begin{equation}
  b_{\rm GW}^2\sum_{\ell}\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}\,
  \mathcal W^{i}_{\ell}\,\mathcal W^{j}_{\ell}\,C_\ell^{gg}(z_i,z_j)
  \;+\;
  b_{\rm GW}b_{\rm gal}\sum_\ell\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}\,
  \mathcal W^i_\ell\,\mathcal W^{g}_\ell\,C_\ell^{\rm GW\times g},
  \label{eq:limit3harmonic}
\end{equation}
which is the harmonic cross-correlation estimator, with the GW localisation
windows $\mathcal W^i_\ell$ playing the role of tomographic bin windows.  The
bias combination that appears, $b_{\rm GW}b_{\rm gal}$, is exactly the
combination a $C_\ell^{\rm GW\times g}$ measurement constrains.
\textbf{Approximate: \eqref{eq:limit3}--\eqref{eq:limit3harmonic} are the
$O(b^2)$ truncation of \eqref{eq:master}, and are exact only in the Gaussian,
weakly-clustered regime.}

\subsection{The unification statement}

Limits I--III are not three methods; they are one likelihood evaluated at three
completeness/localisation regimes.  Catalog dark sirens are the
$\Lambda_{\rm miss}\to0$ corner; cross-correlation is the
$\Lambda_{\rm cat}\to0$, many-cell corner; the field-level hierarchy of
\S\ref{sec:proposed} is the interpolant that is valid at both ends and, unlike
either, in the middle --- which is where every real catalog/detector pairing
actually sits.  The operational content of the unification is
Proposition~\ref{prop:compressexact}: whether a given analysis \emph{needs} the
in-likelihood field is decided by \eqref{eq:requirefullsolve}, a number one can
measure, not by which corner one prefers.

\section{The per-proposal algorithm}
\label{sec:algo}

\subsection{Offline (once per catalog set)}

\begin{verbatim}
ANCHOR BUILD  -- inputs: catalogs {k}, masks {f_k}, anchor (theta_ref, Lambda_ref)
 A1  basis:   Zn, Zz <- inducing nodes (M_sph x M_z);  L <- chol(K_ZZ + j*I)
              Phi_sph (N_pix x M_sph),  Phi_zeta (N_z x M_z)     [never dense Phi]
 A2  counts:  N_kpg <- histogram(z_gal, bins=z_count_edges)      [integer, exact]
 A3  shell response W (G_s x N_fine):
              W[g,n] = (int_g K(z|z_n) dz) * omega_g(z_n; theta_ref) * Delta_n
              rows normalized to 1        [photo-z convolved FORWARD, not on data]
 A4  solve:   xi_hat, H  <- Newton on J_{theta_ref}   [Poisson or multinomial
                                                       grad/Hessian, Sec. 3.6]
 A5  draws:   xi_m = xi_hat + L_H^{-T} g_m,  g_m antithetic pairs, seed stamped
 A6  moments: A_m(z;b) = sum_p e^{b f_m(p,z)},   B_m(z;b) = sum_p f_p e^{b f_m(p,z)}
              on a Chebyshev grid in b;  P = sum_p 1,  F = sum_p f_p
 A7  stamp:   sha256 over (M_sph,M_z,ls_ang,ls_zeta,amp,jitter_mode,j,nside,
              hash(f_p), hash(W), z_count_edges, hash(counts), theta_ref, {b_k})
\end{verbatim}

\noindent Step A6 is what removes the full-sky reduction from the online path.
The budget normaliser with the consumption weights is
\begin{equation}
  \rho_m(z;\theta,b)=\log\frac{\sum_p\big[1-f_pC(z;\theta)\big]e^{b f_m(p,z)}}
                              {\sum_p\big[1-f_pC(z;\theta)\big]}
  =\log\frac{A_m(z;b)-c\,B_m(z;b)}{P-c\,F},
  \qquad c\equiv C(z;\theta),
  \label{eq:rho}
\end{equation}
so the only online $\theta$-dependence is through the \emph{scalar} $c\in[0,1]$
at each $z$.  $A_m,B_m$ are $\theta$-free by construction.  Equation
\eqref{eq:rho} is exact, not an interpolation, given $A_m,B_m$ on the $b$-grid.

\subsection{Online (once per proposal)}

\begin{verbatim}
LIKELIHOOD(H0, Lambda, {theta_k}, {b_k}, b_GW):

 -- decide the path --------------------------------------------------------
 P0  if base is p-separable within every shell        (c_mode in {aggregate,
        AND the frozen-W residual is below budget            selection})
        [the exact-cancellation criterion, Prop. "exact cancellation"]
     then  FAST PATH        (theta-cancellation holds; xi posterior is theta-free)
     else  FULL PATH

 -- FAST PATH: compressed ensemble, exact by Prop. "compressed exactness" --
 F1  c(z)  <- C(z; theta)                                     [scalar per z node]
 F2  rho_m <- (A_m - c B_m) / (P - c F)      closed form, budget normalizer
 F3  for each member m:
       logQ_m(p,z) = b_GW * (row_fac_m[p] . phi_zeta[z]) - log rho_m(z)
       ll_m = PE_terms(logQ_m) - N_ev * log mu(logQ_m)
 F4  logL = LSE_m(ll_m) - log M_draw
 F5  diagnostics: member ESS = exp(-sum_m p_m log p_m),  p_m = softmax(ll_m)

 -- FULL PATH: theta-coupled conditional solve ------------------------------
 G1  xi <- xi_hat(theta_ref)             [DETERMINISTIC anchor, not the previous
                                          proposal's solution unless G3 holds]
 G2  Newton/Fisher-scoring on J_theta:
        grad = xi - b sum_g Phi_g^T (N_g - T_g pi_g)
        H    = I + b^2 sum_g T_g [ Phi_g^T diag(pi_g) Phi_g - u_g u_g^T ]
        (separable two-stage contraction; never form S[p,(i,j)])
 G3  stop when ||grad||_2 < eps  with  eps^2/2  <<  osc-budget
        [Prop. "warm starts are admissible": J(xi*) - J(xi_hat) <= eps^2/2]
 G4  logL_gal = -J_theta(xi_hat) - 0.5*logdet H,  logdet H = 2 sum log diag(L_H)
 G5  draws:  xi_m = xi_hat + L_H^{-T} g_m       [g_m FIXED across theta: CRN]
 G6  logL = logL_gal + LSE_m(ll_m(xi_m)) - log M_draw

 -- the exactness switch ---------------------------------------------------
 S1  monitor  d2 = || xi_hat(theta) - xi_hat(theta_ref) ||_H^2
 S2  if d2 > log(M_draw / ESS_min)   -> FAST PATH is invalid, use FULL PATH
     [the ESS law; note d2 is a SUM over all M modes, NOT a per-mode average,
      so a "0.1 posterior sd per mode" pin means d2 = 0.01*M and fails]
\end{verbatim}

\subsection{Cost structure of the two paths}

The fast path adds work only to the member-vmapped slice: the seam replaces a
gather \verb|member_logq[pix,idx]| by a contraction
\verb|row_fac[pix] @ phi_zeta[idx]|.  The row factors are
$M_{\rm draw}\times n_{\rm rows}\times M_z$ instead of
$M_{\rm draw}\times n_{\rm rows}\times N_{\rm grid}$ --- a compression factor
$N_{\rm grid}/M_z\simeq90$, which is what makes a defensible $M_{\rm draw}$
affordable at all under \eqref{eq:Mrequired}.  The full path adds, per
proposal, one $M\times M$ Cholesky ($M^3/3$) plus the separable contraction
$2G_sN_{\rm pix}M_{\rm sph}^2+2M_{\rm sph}^2G_sM_z^2$; at
$(M_{\rm sph},M_z,G_s)=(315,12,32)$ this is $O(1)$\,s of dense linear algebra
per proposal, i.e. four orders of magnitude above the measured $27.5$--$49.3$\,ms
likelihood baseline.  \textbf{The full path is not affordable inside a
$10^6$-call nested-sampling run at production rank}; it is affordable at
reduced rank, on a grid, or as a validation reference.  This asymmetry --- not
elegance --- is why Proposition~\ref{prop:cancel} is the load-bearing result.
