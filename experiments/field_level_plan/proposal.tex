\section*{Executive summary}
% =====================================================================

The dark-siren likelihood in this repository already contains a three-dimensional
Bayesian reconstruction of the galaxy density field. It is used in the wrong
place. The field is fitted once, offline, at a fixed cosmology, a fixed mean
density and a fixed completeness fit; it is exported to disk as a table
$Q_p(z)$; and a provenance firewall then \emph{forbids} the inference from
sampling any of the parameters the table was built at. The literal sentence
``every cosmological proposal changes the galaxy-field likelihood consistently''
is, today, unrepresentable by construction.

\paragraph{The proposal.} Delete the table. Promote the whitened low-rank
coefficients $\xi$ of the field to a latent variable of the hierarchy, so that
one field appears in three places at once: in the galaxy \emph{counts}, which
constrain it; in the \emph{placement} of missing hosts, which the gravitational-wave
events probe; and in the \emph{selection normalisation}, which decides what
fraction of universes like this one are detectable. Every cosmological proposal
then re-weights the galaxy-field likelihood, and the missing-host field it
delivers to the event terms and to the detected fraction is the same realisation.

\paragraph{Why this is more than plumbing.} The same generative hierarchy
contains the two established dark-siren analyses as limits of one likelihood.
When the catalog is complete the missing branch vanishes and the field drops out
\emph{identically}: one recovers the discrete weighted-host sum. When the
catalogued fraction of an event's localisation volume tends to zero and the
volume spans many correlation cells, marginalising the Gaussian latent
analytically replaces the field by its kernel, and the surviving
event$\,\times\,$galaxy-voxel term is the harmonic cross-correlation
$C_\ell^{\rm GW\times g}$ carrying the bias combination $\bgw\bgal$. The interior
--- an incomplete catalog with well-localised events --- is where every real
catalog/detector pairing actually sits, and it is the regime neither limit
describes. Which corner a given pairing occupies is set by two numbers one can
measure, not by which method one prefers.

\paragraph{What is affordable, and what is not.} A full per-proposal Newton
re-solve of the field is not affordable: at the smallest rank the resolution
guards permit ($M=\Msph\Mz=315\times12=3780$) it is $\sim1$\,s of dense linear
algebra against a measured $27.5$--$49.3$\,ms likelihood, a factor of $\sim36$,
inside a run that makes $\sim10^6$ calls. The affordable form is
\emph{linear response}: the mode of the field posterior moves affinely with the
proposal through $S=\partial\hat\xi/\partial\theta$, obtained from the implicit
function theorem against the Cholesky factor the anchor solve already computes,
at a storage cost of $121$\,kB and an online cost of one member-independent
row-factor contraction. The correction is member-independent, which is the single
fact that keeps the memory budget flat under a $256$-fold evaluation concurrency.

\paragraph{What must be conceded in the same breath.} Three things, and they
belong in the abstract, not the appendix. (i) The galaxy field constrains the
\emph{shape} parameters $(\Omega_m,w_0,w_a,\delta)$ and the tracer biases; it
constrains $H_0$ only through the volume--density amplitude $n_0(c/H_0)^3$, which
is exactly degenerate with the mean comoving number density --- so an $H_0$
constraint from the counts is manufactured unless $n_0$ carries an $H_0$-aware
prior. (ii) The field is defined over the volume the survey sees: $Q\equiv1$ off
the footprint ($38\%$ of the sky) and above the survey depth $\zdep=0.3$. Four
independent integrals put the in-support fraction of the missing-galaxy budget at
$2.2\times10^{-5}$ to $2.7\times10^{-4}$. The lowest and most defensible is the
verification integral for this document, which uses the shipped fit
\emph{including its cubic $K(z)$ template} on the production $\zmaxsix$ grid:
$2.2\times10^{-5}$ all-sky, $1.4\times10^{-5}$ restricted to the occupied
footprint. The $K$-correction is why it sits below the other three: it drives
$\Csel$ down faster with redshift ($\Csel=0.92$ at $\zdep$, $<10^{-3}$ by
$z=1$), moving yet more of the missing budget above the survey depth, where
$Q\equiv1$. The field is being asked to redistribute two thousandths of one
percent of the missing budget. (iii) The mean-one budget
constraint that makes ``$C$ and $n_0$ own the budget, $Q$ owns placement'' true is
a \emph{gauge fixing}, not a measurement.

\paragraph{The first thing to do is the cheapest thing to do.} Point (ii) is not
a caveat, it is a falsifiable prediction that the whole direction may be a
null operation, and it is testable in three days against artefacts that exist
today: the event-weighted fraction of prior mass in the in-support missing branch,
and the oscillation of the log-likelihood across the $H_0$ prior when the
\emph{existing} table is switched on at the anchor. That second number is an upper
bound on everything the entire programme can buy. If both are small, the honest
deliverable is a bounded systematic --- ``large-scale-structure placement
uncertainty contributes less than $X$ to the $H_0$ posterior'' --- and it is a
publishable result reachable in days rather than weeks. Every other measurement in
this document is downstream of that one.

\paragraph{What is genuinely new, stated at the level the literature permits.}
Three-dimensional Bayesian reconstruction of the galaxy field for dark sirens is
not new. Lognormal density reconstruction is not new. Multiplicative
large-scale-structure corrections to catalog completion are not new. What is not
found in the completion literature is the conjunction: a \emph{shared} latent
field carrying per-tracer bias and per-tracer parametric selection, marginalised
inside a finite-event gravitational-wave hierarchy with the same realisation in
the event terms and in the detection normalisation, at fixed missing-budget
monopole --- and the demonstration that catalog sirens and cross-correlation
sirens are its two limits. The claim to \emph{first} is not made here; the
paper-by-paper search that would license it is the first item of work.

\section{What the machinery does today}
\label{sec:today}
% =====================================================================

The pieces are usually described as three machines --- a builder, a table, a
likelihood. They are one generative model, and writing them as one is what
exposes where the model is exact and where it is a plug-in. All equations marked
\textsc{[code]} are transcribed from verified source lines at \texttt{master}
\texttt{0c5b3db}.

\subsection{Notation}

Sky pixels are $p\in\{1,\dots,\Npix\}$ in HEALPix RING ordering at
$N_{\rm side}=64$ ($\Npix=49\,152$, $\Omega_{\rm pix}=4\pi/\Npix$); redshift is
carried on a grid of $N_{\rm grid}=1086$ nodes; a voxel is $v=(p,z)$; coarse
radial shells used by the field fit are $g\in\{1,\dots,G_s\}$; catalogs (tracers)
are $k\in\{1,\dots,K\}$. Cosmological and population parameters are $\Lambda$
(containing $H_0$); survey parameters are
$\theta=(\log_{10}n_0,\delta,\theta_{\rm sel},b_{\rm miss},\dots)$. The expected
all-galaxy comoving density is
\begin{equation}
  \nbar(z;\Lambda,\theta)\;=\;\frac{dN_{\rm exp}}{dz\,d\Omega}
  \;=\;n_0\,(1+z)^{\delta}\,\frac{dV_c}{dz\,d\Omega}(z;\Lambda),
  \label{eq:dNexp}
\end{equation}
and $C(z;\theta)\in[0,1]$ is the completeness. The production configuration uses
the parametric $C_{\rm sel}(z;\theta_{\rm sel})$ built from a truncated luminosity
function; $f_p=1-\texttt{masked\_frac}_p\in[0,1]$ is the per-pixel areal
completeness, which is $f_p\equiv1$ in today's code.

The production run this work targets carries $259$ events at $4096$ posterior
samples, $1\,067\,946$ detected injections, $20$ free dimensions, and
\emph{no large-scale structure at all}: it runs with the field switched off and no
completion table. The field-level path would be the first structure ever used in
production, which is why the standard is ``provably inert when off, provably
correct when on.''

\subsection{Thinning: why the catalog and the missing galaxies separate}

Conditional on a latent log-density contrast $s(p,z)$, galaxies are an
inhomogeneous Poisson process with the lognormal intensity
\begin{equation}
  \Lgal(p,z)\;=\;\nbar(z;\Lambda,\theta)\,
  \exp\!\Big[\bgal\,s(p,z)-\tfrac12\bgal^2\sigma^2(p,z)\Big],
  \qquad \sigma^2(p,z)\equiv\Var[s(p,z)],
  \label{eq:galintensity}
\end{equation}
where the shift $-\tfrac12 b^2\sigma^2$ enforces
$\mathbb E_{\rm prior}[e^{bs-\frac12b^2\sigma^2}]=1$: the field \emph{redistributes}
galaxies, it does not create them. This mean-one convention is the most
load-bearing convention in the stack and it reappears three times below.

The survey observes each galaxy independently with probability $f_pC(z;\theta)$.
By the Poisson thinning theorem the catalogued and missing galaxies are two
\emph{independent} Poisson processes,
\begin{align}
  \Lcat(p,z) &= f_p\,C(z;\theta)\,\Lgal(p,z), \label{eq:thincat}\\
  \Lmiss(p,z) &= \big[1-f_p\,C(z;\theta)\big]\,\Lgal(p,z),
  \label{eq:thinmiss}
\end{align}
and, exactly,
\begin{equation}
  p\big(\text{missing}\,\big|\,\text{catalogued},\,s\big)
  \;=\;p\big(\text{missing}\,\big|\,s\big).
  \label{eq:thinindep}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:thinindep} says: once the field is fixed, the catalog carries
no further information about what is missing. Everything the catalog knows about
the missing galaxies, it knows through $s$. It is the entire resolution of the
double-conditioning question of \S\ref{sec:double}.

\subsection{The redshift--sky prior as it is coded}

The likelihood does not integrate \eqref{eq:thincat}; it uses the catalog itself.
For a dark siren at $(z,p)$,
\begin{equation}
  p\big(z,p\,\big|\,\Lambda,\theta,Q\big)
  \;=\;\frac{N_{{\rm obs},p}\,\mathcal K_p(z)\;+\;\big[1-C(z;\theta)\big]\,
  \nbar(z;\Lambda,\theta)\;Q_p(z)}{\mathcal Z(\Lambda,\theta,Q)},
  \label{eq:zprior}
\end{equation}
\textsc{[code]} with $N_{{\rm obs},p}$ the integer galaxy count in pixel $p$ and
$\mathcal K_p$ the pixel's photometric-redshift kernel density. The consumed
missing density is $dN_{\rm miss}=[1-C]\,\nbar\,Q$, relaxed to $dN_{\rm exp}$ above
the survey depth $\zdep$ --- i.e.\ $C:=0$, $Q:=1$ there. Comparing with
\eqref{eq:thinmiss} identifies
\begin{equation}
  Q_p(z)\;\equiv\;\exp\!\Big[b_{\rm miss}\,s(p,z)-\tfrac12 b_{\rm miss}^2\sigma^2(p,z)\Big]
  \quad\text{with } f_p\equiv1 .
  \label{eq:Qdef}
\end{equation}
So $Q$ \emph{is} the exponentiated latent field, mean-one normalised, and nothing
else. Its only defect as written is that it is a fixed table read from disk rather
than a function of the sampled parameters.

Two structural consequences of $f_p\equiv1$ deserve naming, because both are
first-order for the target survey. The measured areal masking has mean $0.137$,
standard deviation $0.104$ and $99$th percentile $0.635$ over the occupied
footprint, while the magnitude depth is uniform at the retention cut
($m_{\rm th}$ percentiles $1$, $50$, $99$ all equal $21.000$; the imaging depth is
nowhere the limit). So the entire per-pixel selection variation is \emph{areal},
which is achromatic in redshift and magnitude and therefore exactly correctable.
First, putting $f_p$ on the count side only would invert the sign of the inferred
incompleteness: a pixel with $63\%$ areal masking would be told that $63\%$ of its
galaxies are a \emph{density deficit} rather than a \emph{selection deficit}, and
that deficit re-enters the event likelihood as a claim about where hosts are not.
Second, the dominant term is not the partial pixels: it is the $18\,682$ of
$49\,152$ pixels with $f_p=0$, whose missing-budget weight moves from $1-C$ to $1$,
because a pixel with no catalog has \emph{all} of its galaxies missing.

\subsection{The offline field: Poisson--lognormal on a low-rank basis}

Let $\{Z_j\}_{j=1}^M$ be inducing nodes on $\mathbb S^2\times\zeta$,
$\zeta=\log(1+z)$ --- a Fibonacci sphere of $\Msph$ points crossed with $\Mz$ nodes
uniform in $\zeta$ --- and $k=k_{\rm sph}\otimes k_\zeta$ a separable kernel. With
$LL^{\!\top}=K_{ZZ}+j\mathbb 1$, the Nystr\"om design and the whitened field are
\begin{equation}
  \Phi=K_{XZ}L^{-\!\top}\in\mathbb R^{V\times M},
  \qquad s=\Phi\xi,\qquad \xi\sim\mathcal N(0,\mathbb 1_M),
  \label{eq:lowrank}
\end{equation}
so $\mathrm{Cov}(s)=\Phi\Phi^{\!\top}$ and $\sigma^2_{\rm prior}(v)=\sum_i\Phi_{vi}^2$.
$\Phi$ factorises exactly as $\Phi_{\rm sph}\otimes\Phi_\zeta$ and is never
materialised: a dense $\Phi$ at $M=3780$ would be $47.6$\,GB, and a build at
$M=1728$ has already exhausted memory at $21.7$\,GB.

The rank is not free. The angular and radial lengthscales must be equal in
comoving units or the kernel is a pancake, and the radial scale must survive the
photometric-redshift scatter. The measured scatter is $\sigma_z=0.0227$ (median
over $2.24$M galaxies), i.e.\ $\sigma_\chi=89$\,Mpc at $z_{\rm ref}=0.237$, so a
$50$\,Mpc radial kernel retains $\exp[-(89/50)^2/2]=0.20$ of its own radial signal
while a $190$\,Mpc kernel retains $0.90$. The self-consistent configuration is
isotropic at $\sim190$\,Mpc: $\ell_{\rm ang}=0.2$, $\ell_\zeta=0.039$, $\Msph=315$,
$\Mz=8$--$12$, $M=2520$--$3780$. At that rank roughly $40\%$ of the coefficients
are data-constrained and the remainder are prior --- which is correct, and is what
the Laplace covariance encodes.

The fit is over solve voxels $v=(p,g)$ with integer counts $N_v$ and a base
$\bar\lambda_v=\int_g C\,\nbar\,dz$, giving
\begin{equation}
  J(\xi)=\tfrac12\|\xi\|^2+\sum_v\big[\lambda_v-N_v\log\lambda_v\big],
  \qquad
  \lambda_v=\bar\lambda_v\exp\!\Big[b(\Phi\xi)_v-\tfrac12b^2\sigma^2_{\rm prior}(v)\Big],
  \label{eq:Jpoisson}
\end{equation}
\textsc{[code]} with $\nabla J=\xi+b\Phi^{\!\top}(\lambda-N)$ and
$H=\mathbb 1+b^2\Phi^{\!\top}\mathrm{diag}(\lambda)\Phi\succeq\mathbb 1$. The problem
is convex and well posed even when $V<M$. The Laplace posterior
$\mathcal N(\hat\xi,H^{-1})$ is sampled as
\begin{equation}
  \xi_m=\hat\xi+L_H^{-\!\top}g_m,\qquad g_m\sim\mathcal N(0,\mathbb 1_M),
  \qquad L_HL_H^{\!\top}=H .
  \label{eq:laplacedraws}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[posterior-mean $Q$ and its variance shift]
\label{lem:varshift}
Under \eqref{eq:laplacedraws}, for the Nystr\"om row $\phi_u$ at an output voxel
$u$,
\begin{equation}
  \log\mathbb E[Q_u]
  =b\,\phi_u^{\!\top}\hat\xi-\tfrac12b^2\big[\sigma^2_{\rm prior}(u)-\sigma^2_{\rm post}(u)\big],
  \qquad \sigma^2_{\rm post}(u)=\|L_H^{-1}\phi_u\|^2 .
  \label{eq:postmeanQ}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\noindent Equation \eqref{eq:postmeanQ} \emph{is} the table the likelihood consumes
today. It has a physically desirable property that a MAP table does not: a voxel
with no data has $\sigma_{\rm post}\to\sigma_{\rm prior}$ and
$\phi_u^{\!\top}\hat\xi\to0$, so $Q\to1$. \emph{No information, no modulation.}

\begin{remark}[the plug-in gap, stated exactly]
\label{rem:plugin}
The event likelihood is a nonlinear functional of $Q$: $Q$ appears inside a
logarithm in \eqref{eq:zprior} and inside $\log\mu$ in the detection
normalisation. Hence $\log\mathbb E_\xi[\mathcal L(Q(\xi))]\neq
\log\mathcal L(\mathbb E_\xi[Q])$, and running the deterministic table is a
plug-in, exact only to first order in the posterior spread of $s$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The mean-one budget constraint is a gauge fixing}

\begin{proposition}[$Q$ has no monopole freedom]
\label{prop:gauge}
For any positive $\alpha(z)$, the map $Q_p(z)\mapsto\alpha(z)Q_p(z)$ together with
$[1-C]\nbar\mapsto[1-C]\nbar/\alpha(z)$ leaves $dN_{\rm miss}$, and therefore the
entire event likelihood, exactly invariant. The per-$z$ monopole of $Q$ is
therefore not identified by the gravitational-wave data; it is identified only by
whatever fixes $(n_0,\delta,\theta_{\rm sel})$.
\end{proposition}

The code fixes this gauge at build time by rescaling each realisation so that
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\sum_{p\in\mathcal F}w_p(z)\,Q_p(z)}{\sum_{p\in\mathcal F}w_p(z)}=1,
  \qquad w_p(z)=\big[1-C_p(z)\big]\,\nbar(z),
  \label{eq:renorm}
\end{equation}
over the \emph{fitted footprint} $\mathcal F$, applied
to the deterministic table and to \emph{each member independently}. This is
necessary and it is measured: the Jensen contribution of
Lemma~\ref{lem:varshift} otherwise inflates the missing budget by $+55\%$, because
the posterior variance is largest exactly where the data are sparse.

Two precisions matter for what may be claimed. First, in the production
completeness modes the constraint is \emph{already} exactly the identity the
likelihood consumes: the builder fits the whole sky with $p$-independent weights,
so mean-one reduces to $\langle Q\rangle_{\rm sky}=1$ and the consumed sum equals
its $Q\equiv1$ value for every $z$, member and $\theta$. Under \emph{stratified}
selection the weights are $p$-dependent and evaluated at the build-time fit, so
that identity holds only at $\theta=\theta_{\rm ref}$ and leaks elsewhere at the
order of the stratum-to-stratum spread in $C_{\rm sel}$. Second, and more
importantly for the paper: by Proposition~\ref{prop:gauge} this is a
\emph{convention that makes an estimand well posed}, i.e.\ a definition, not a
measurement. ``$C$ and $n_0$ own the budget, $Q$ owns placement'' is the correct
sentence, and ``gauge fixing'' is the correct word for it.

\begin{remark}[renormalisation does not commute with the ensemble mean]
\label{rem:noncommute}
Writing $\Pi$ for the renormalisation, $\Pi$ is nonlinear in $Q$, so
$\frac1M\sum_m\Pi[Q^{(m)}]\neq\Pi[\mathbb E[Q]]$ at order
$\Var_{\rm sky}(Q)/\Npix$ per shell. ``Members carry placement uncertainty but zero
budget uncertainty'' is therefore a modelling choice, not a theorem.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Member marginalisation, and the price of a logarithm}

With $\Mdraw$ realisations the likelihood is
\begin{equation}
  \log\mathcal L(\Lambda,\theta)=\LSE_m\log\mathcal L_m-\log\Mdraw,
  \label{eq:membermarg}
\end{equation}
\textsc{[code]} where the member index enters \emph{both} the per-event terms and
the detection normalisation $\mu$ --- enforced as an all-or-none guard, and
correct: a member is one realisation of the universe, and the same universe must
place the hosts and set the detected fraction.

\begin{proposition}[Jensen bias of \eqref{eq:membermarg}]
\label{prop:jensen}
\eqref{eq:membermarg} is unbiased for $\mathcal L$ and biased for
$\log\mathcal L$. If $\log\mathcal L_m$ has spread $\sigma$ nats and is
approximately Gaussian across members, then
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb E\big[\log\widehat{\mathcal L}_M\big]-\log\mathcal L
  \simeq-\frac{e^{\sigma^2}-1}{2M},
  \qquad \frac{\ESS}{M}\simeq e^{-\sigma^2},
  \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
  \boxed{\,M>\frac{e^{\sigma^2}-1}{2\epsilon}\,}
  \label{eq:Mrequired}
\end{equation}
for an $\epsilon$-nat bias.
\end{proposition}

\noindent This is sharper than the usual $M\sim e^{\sigma^2}$ folklore and it is
brutal. At $\sigma=1.0$ nats, $\epsilon=0.1$ needs $M=9$; at $\sigma=1.5$, $M=43$;
at $\sigma=2.6$, $M=4.3\times10^{3}$, and $M=8$ carries a $54$-nat bias. The member
spread is therefore the largest open number in the design, and \S\ref{sec:est}
predicts it in closed form rather than discovering it.

\begin{remark}[what actually has to be gated]
\label{rem:crn}
The whitened draws $g_m$ are generated once and reused at every $\theta$ (common
random numbers), so $\widehat{\mathcal L}_M(\theta)$ is a \emph{deterministic,
smooth} function of $\theta$, not a noisy one. The bias of
Proposition~\ref{prop:jensen} is a fixed perturbation and only its
$\theta$-variation,
$\osc_\theta\Delta\equiv\max_\theta\Delta-\min_\theta\Delta$ with
$\Delta(\theta)=\log\widehat{\mathcal L}_M-\log\mathcal L$, distorts the posterior;
a constant offset is absorbed into the evidence. The consequence is unforgiving:
every determinism diagnostic --- repeat-to-repeat identity, adjacent-$\theta$
smoothness --- passes \emph{by construction}, and passes just as well on a badly
distorted surrogate. Only $\osc_\theta\Delta$ against a large-$M$ reference
discriminates.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The shared-latent multitracer solve, and which half of the seam is open}
\label{sec:seam}

For $K$ catalogs sharing one field the stacked objective and Hessian are
\begin{equation}
  J(\xi)=\tfrac12\|\xi\|^2+\sum_{k}\sum_{v\in\mathcal V_k}
  \big[\lambda_{kv}-N_{kv}\log\lambda_{kv}\big],
  \qquad
  H=\mathbb 1+\sum_k b_k^2\,\Phi_k^{\!\top}\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_k)\Phi_k ,
  \label{eq:jointJ}
\end{equation}
with $\lambda_{kv}=\bar\lambda_{kv}e^{\,b_k(\Phi_k\xi)_v-\frac12b_k^2\sigma^2_{kv}}$,
one Laplace ensemble, and per-survey tables
$\log Q_k^{(m)}=b_k\phi^{\!\top}\xi_m-\frac12b_k^2\sigma^2_{\rm prior}$. Member $m$
of every catalog is then the same physical realisation --- a theorem, not a check.

The interesting statement is where this stands operationally, and it is not where
one would guess. \textbf{The consumer is ready; the producer cannot produce.} On
the inference side, $K\ge2$ mixtures under parametric selection completeness
assemble and evaluate end to end: per-catalog completeness modes, per-catalog
selection fits with an all-or-nothing anchoring rule, a homogeneous
luminosity-function family, per-catalog parameter routing, and a two-catalog
end-to-end likelihood fixture pinning the identical-catalogs-collapse-to-$K{=}1$
identity. Exactly one refusal survives on that side --- \emph{stratified} fits at
$K\ge2$ --- and its stated reason is data plumbing, not modelling.

On the builder side the shared-field path is closed in four independent ways. It
takes the per-pixel completeness default and is hard-rejected at load by any
parametric-selection run; it never fixes the budget gauge, so every joint file
ships the full $+55\%$ Jensen monopole that the single-catalog tables remove; its
inducing grid is hard-coded at $\Msph,\Mz=32,6$ up to $z=3$, giving a $\zeta$ node
spacing of $0.277$ and hence a \emph{hard} floor of $L_{\rm smooth}\ge1.34$\,Gpc ---
every physically supportable correlation length fails it; and it is the only
producer of a shared realisation identifier, so $K$ independently built
single-catalog tables carry $K$ different ones. The only way to run the
configuration is a flag whose own documentation states that it marginalises over
an \emph{independent-fields product prior} rather than the shared-field prior the
estimator assumes.

\begin{center}\itshape
A shared-latent multitracer marginalisation under parametric selection
completeness is not refused. It is not constructible.
\end{center}

\noindent This is the cleanest one-line motivation for the whole proposal: the
field-level hierarchy closes that seam \emph{by deleting the producer}. One $\xi$
shared across $K$ tracers makes matched realisations a theorem, retires the
realisation identifier and the provenance firewall together, and removes the
builder's frozen rank, frozen cosmology and frozen completeness mode
simultaneously.

\subsection{The hierarchy}

Promote $\xi$ from a table to a latent variable:
\begin{multline}
  p\big(H_0,\Lambda,\xi,\{\theta_k\},\{b_k\},\bgw,\{f_k\}\;\big|\;\dgw,\{d_{{\rm gal},k}\}\big)
  \;\propto\;
  \pi(H_0,\Lambda)\,\pi(\{\theta_k\})\,\pi(\{b_k\})\,\pi(\bgw)\\
  \times\;\underbrace{\mathcal N(\xi;0,\mathbb 1_M)}_{\rm (F1)}\;
  \times\;\prod_{k}\underbrace{p\big(\{m_i\}_k\,\big|\,\{z_i\}_k,\theta_k\big)}_{\rm (F2)\ magnitudes}\;
  \times\;\prod_{k}\underbrace{p\big(\{N_{kv}\}\,\big|\,\xi,\theta_k,b_k,f_k,\Lambda\big)}_{\rm (F3)\ counts}\\
  \times\;\underbrace{p\big(\dgw\,\big|\,\{d_{{\rm gal},k}\},\xi,\bgw,\Lambda,\{\theta_k\}\big)}_{\rm (F4)\ GW}
  \label{eq:hierarchy}
\end{multline}
The single structural change is that $\xi$ now appears in (F1), (F3) \emph{and}
(F4).

\paragraph{An honest statement about (F2).} In the shipped pipeline the magnitude
channel is \emph{not} a likelihood factor: the magnitude log-likelihood is defined
but referenced nowhere outside its own test, and $\theta_{\rm sel}$ enters as an
anchored Gaussian prior whose width comes from the offline fit's covariance
($\sigma(\hat M_0)=1.60\times10^{-4}$\,mag). The shipped hierarchy is therefore
empirical Bayes on $\theta_{\rm sel}$, and \eqref{eq:hierarchy} is the target, not
the current object. This matters exactly once: at the unconditional rung of
\S\ref{sec:cancel} the restored shell totals draw $\theta_{\rm sel}$ information
from the same magnitudes that produced the prior, and the combination is refused.

\subsection{(F3): the count likelihood, and the one approximation that buys three things}
\label{sec:counts}

On solve voxels $v=(p,g)$,
\begin{equation}
  N_{kv}\;\sim\;\mathrm{Poisson}\Big[
  \underbrace{f_{k,p}\,C_k(v;\theta_k)\,\bar N_k(v;\Lambda,\theta_k)}_{\bar\lambda_{kv}}
  \;\exp\!\big(b_ks_v-\tfrac12b_k^2\sigma_v^2\big)\Big],
  \label{eq:countlike}
\end{equation}
with $\bar N_k(v)=\Omega_{\rm pix}\int_g\nbar\,dz$. Two consequences must be stated
because both change the completeness side. $f_{k,p}$ appears here \emph{and} in the
consumed missing budget $dN_{\rm miss}=[1-f_pC]\,\nbar\,Q$ --- one side only inverts
the sign of the inferred incompleteness. And under a per-pixel completeness the
base $\bar\lambda_{kv}$ is itself a plug-in estimate of the same counts, so
\eqref{eq:countlike} would be circular; only bases that are functions of $\theta$
rather than of the counts are admissible.

Conditioning on the per-shell totals $T_{kg}=\sum_pN_{kpg}$ factorises the counts
exactly,
\begin{equation}
  p\big(\{N_{kpg}\}\,\big|\,\xi,\theta\big)
  =\underbrace{p\big(\{T_{kg}\}\,\big|\,\xi,\theta\big)}_{\rm monopole}
  \times\underbrace{p\big(\{N_{kpg}\}\,\big|\,\{T_{kg}\},\xi,\theta\big)}_{\rm placement},
  \label{eq:shellfactor}
\end{equation}
and using only the second factor is a deliberate \emph{partial likelihood}: the
factorisation is exact, the choice is not free. The discarded monopole is removed
to linear order by an explicit projection of the sky-constant coefficients and
retains $O(b^2\Var_{\rm sky}(s)/2)$ content.

\paragraph{The within-shell linearisation.} Write the shell-integrated intensity
and its normalised within-shell profile,
\begin{equation}
  \Lambda_{pg}=a_g(\theta)\,f_p\!\int_g\omega_g(z;\theta)\,
  e^{\,bs_p(z)-\frac12b^2\sigma_p^2(z)}dz,
  \qquad
  \omega_g(z;\theta)=\frac{C\nbar}{\int_gC\nbar\,dz},
  \label{eq:Lambdapg}
\end{equation}
with $a_g$ $p$-independent. Expanding the integral,
$\log\int_g\omega_ge^{bs_p}dz=b\langle s_p\rangle_g+\tfrac12b^2\Var_g(s_p)+O(b^3)$,
and \emph{keeping the first term} defines the shell-response operator $W$ to act on
the radial \emph{basis rows} rather than around the exponential:
\begin{equation}
  \tilde\phi_\zeta[g,:]=\sum_nW_{gn}\,\phi_\zeta[n,:],
  \qquad
  W_{gn}=\Big(\int_g\mathcal K(z|z_n)\,dz\Big)\,\omega_g(z_n;\theta)\,\Delta_n,
  \label{eq:Wdef}
\end{equation}
rows normalised to unity, so that
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;
  \eta_{pg}=b\,\big(\Phi_{\rm sph}[p]\otimes\tilde\phi_\zeta[g]\big)\cdot\xi,
  \qquad
  \pi_{pg}=\frac{f_p\,e^{\eta_{pg}}}{\sum_{p'}f_{p'}e^{\eta_{p'g}}},
  \qquad
  \log p_{\rm count}=\sum_g\sum_pN_{pg}\log\pi_{pg}. \;}
  \label{eq:pipg}
\end{equation}

This is the load-bearing modelling step, and it is worth being explicit about what
it buys and what it costs. \emph{Buys:} $\eta$ is exactly linear in $\xi$, so the
Kronecker factorisation $\Phi_g=\Phi_{\rm sph}\otimes\tilde\phi_\zeta[g]$ is exact,
the Hessian below is exact, and the sky-constant subspace is an \emph{exact} gauge
direction of \eqref{eq:pipg}. \emph{Costs:} the neglected term is precisely
$\tfrac12b^2[\Var_g(s_p)-\langle\sigma_p^2\rangle_g]$, a within-shell variance,
which is $p$-dependent (which is why it does not divide out) and which vanishes
identically when $s$ is constant across a shell. \emph{And this is the same
condition that makes the completeness parameters cancel} (Prop.~\ref{prop:cancel}
below): one approximation controls the separability, the exactness of the Hessian,
and the $\theta$-cancellation together, and its controlling scale is the shell
width against the photometric-redshift-smoothed radial structure. Moving $W$ onto
the basis is also what makes it a genuine forward model: the photometric-redshift
kernel is convolved into the \emph{model}, not deconvolved from the data, so the
counts remain integer, the multinomial remains exact, and the radial attenuation
$\sim e^{-k^2\sigma_\chi^2/2}$ does not bias the tracer bias.

\paragraph{Gradient and Hessian.} For \eqref{eq:pipg},
\begin{align}
  \nabla J_\theta &= \xi-b\sum_g\Phi_g^{\!\top}\big(N_g-T_g\pi_g\big),
  \label{eq:multgrad}\\
  H(\theta) &= \mathbb 1+b^2\sum_gT_g\Big[\Phi_g^{\!\top}\mathrm{diag}(\pi_g)\Phi_g
              -u_gu_g^{\!\top}\Big],\qquad u_g=\Phi_g^{\!\top}\pi_g .
  \label{eq:multhess}
\end{align}
The rank-one subtraction is not optional: it is positive semidefinite, so omitting
it makes $H$ too large, $H^{-1}$ too small, and the Laplace draws
\emph{under}-dispersed --- exactly the direction that hides the error of
Proposition~\ref{prop:jensen}. It is separable and therefore free:
$u_g=v_g\otimes\tilde\phi_\zeta[g]$ with $v_g=\Phi_{\rm sph}^{\!\top}\pi_g$.
Equation \eqref{eq:multhess} is the \emph{Fisher} information of the multinomial,
hence $H\succeq\mathbb 1$ for any link function; using the observed Hessian instead
would forfeit that guarantee under a saturating link, so Fisher scoring is
normative rather than convenient.

\subsection{(F4): the gravitational-wave host intensity on the same field}

Hosts are built from the \emph{same} $s$ with their own bias,
\begin{equation}
  \Lhost(p,z;\Lambda,\theta,\xi)=\psi(z;\Lambda)\,\nbar(z;\Lambda,\theta)\,
  \exp\!\Big[\bgw s(p,z)-\tfrac12\bgw^2\sigma^2(p,z)\Big],
  \label{eq:hostintensity}
\end{equation}
with $\psi$ the merger-rate weighting. Thinning \eqref{eq:hostintensity} by the same
$f_pC$ gives the catalogued/missing split. The point that a
cross-correlation-literate reader checks first is the \emph{catalogued} branch:
since hosts trace $e^{\bgw s}$ while galaxies trace $e^{\bgal s}$, the probability
that a specific catalogued galaxy $j$ hosts the event carries an excess-bias
factor. The correct prior is
\begin{multline}
  p\big(z,p\,\big|\,\mathcal C,\xi,\Lambda,\theta\big)
  \;=\;\frac{1}{\mathcal Z(\Lambda,\theta,\xi)}
   \Bigg[\;\sum_{j\in\mathcal C}\delta_{p,p_j}\,\mathcal K_j(z)\,w_j(\Lambda)\,
        e^{\,(\bgw-\bgal)s(x_j)-\frac12(\bgw^2-\bgal^2)\sigma^2(x_j)}\\[2pt]
   +\;\big[1-f_p\,C(z;\theta)\big]\,\psi(z;\Lambda)\,\nbar(z;\Lambda,\theta)\;
     e^{\,\bgw s(p,z)-\frac12\bgw^2\sigma^2(p,z)}\Bigg].
  \label{eq:zpriornew}
\end{multline}
At $\bgw=\bgal$ the spike factor is identically unity and the catalogued branch is
field-free; away from it, writing the branch field-free is a modelling error that
also makes the complete-catalog limit below true by omission rather than by
physics. Setting $\bgw\equiv\bgal$ in a headline analysis is therefore not only a
statistics choice; it is the choice that makes Limit I exact.

Structurally \eqref{eq:zpriornew} is \eqref{eq:zprior} with $Q_p(z)$ replaced by an
explicit function of $\xi$, which is why the change in the evaluation is one
contraction: $\log Q\to\bgw\,(\mathrm{row\_fac}[p]\cdot\tilde\phi_\zeta[n])-\rho$.

\subsection{Double conditioning: the catalog is used once}
\label{sec:double}

\begin{theorem}[no double use]
\label{thm:double}
Let $\mathcal C$ be the catalogued galaxies and $\mathcal U$ the missing ones. Then
exactly
\begin{equation}
  p\big(\mathcal C,\dgw\,\big|\,\xi,\Lambda,\theta\big)
  =\underbrace{p\big(\mathcal C\,\big|\,\xi,\theta,\Lambda\big)}_{\rm (F3)}
  \times\underbrace{p\big(\dgw\,\big|\,\mathcal C,\xi,\Lambda,\theta\big)}_{\rm (F4)},
  \label{eq:chainrule}
\end{equation}
and the second factor may be evaluated with \eqref{eq:zpriornew} without any double
use of the catalog, \emph{provided the missing intensity is written as a function of
$\xi$ alone and never of $\mathcal C$.}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\eqref{eq:chainrule} is the chain rule. The gravitational-wave factor requires the
catalogued hosts --- a conditioning event, used as known positions, not re-modelled
--- and the intensity of $\mathcal U$, which by \eqref{eq:thinindep} is
$[1-f_pC]\Lgal(\xi)$ and involves $\mathcal C$ nowhere. The catalog's information
about $\mathcal U$ has been routed entirely through (F3) into the posterior for
$\xi$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the two ways it breaks, both live]
\label{rem:doublebreak}
(i) \emph{Empirical-Bayes plug-in.} If the missing intensity is evaluated at a
point estimate $\hat\xi(\mathcal C)$ --- which is exactly what the current table is
--- then $\mathcal C$ appears inside the gravitational-wave factor, and multiplying
by (F3) uses the same counts twice. The present code is safe \emph{only} because it
never multiplies by (F3). \textbf{Adding the count likelihood without promoting
$\xi$ to a latent variable is the single most dangerous intermediate state
available, and it must never exist.}
(ii) \emph{The monopole, twice.} The calibration that pins $(\log_{10}n_0,\delta)$
is a fit to the \emph{same} counts over $z\in[0.05,0.3]$, delivered to the run as
fixed values; that prior is a surrogate for the first factor of
\eqref{eq:shellfactor}. Keeping both it \emph{and} an unconditional Poisson count
likelihood squares the monopole information. Exactly two combinations are
admissible: conditional counts $+$ external calibration prior, or unconditional
counts $+$ a genuinely independent prior on $(n_0,\delta)$. The first is
recommended; the second carries \S\ref{sec:n0h0}'s degeneracy.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Which parameters the field is allowed to couple to}
\label{sec:cancel}

\begin{proposition}[exact cancellation]
\label{prop:cancel}
$\pi_{pg}$ is exactly independent of $\theta$ and $\Lambda$ if and only if the base
is $p$-separable within the shell --- true whenever $C$ is sky-uniform --- and the
field is shell-constant. Otherwise the residual is exactly a within-shell
covariance,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial\log\pi_{pg}}{\partial\theta}
  =\frac{\int_g\partial_\theta\omega_g\,e^{bs_p}dz}{\int_g\omega_g\,e^{bs_p}dz}
  -\sum_{p'}\pi_{p'g}\,
  \frac{\int_g\partial_\theta\omega_g\,e^{bs_{p'}}dz}{\int_g\omega_g\,e^{bs_{p'}}dz},
  \label{eq:cancelresidual}
\end{equation}
which vanishes identically when $e^{bs_p(z)}$ is constant across the shell.
\end{proposition}

\noindent This is the specification of the coupling set, and it is exact. Since
$dV_c/dz=(c/H_0)^3\times\text{shape}(z;\Omega_m)$ and the completeness curve is
built $h$-free, $H_0$ cancels from \eqref{eq:pipg} identically while
$\Omega_m,w_0,w_a,\delta$ and $\theta_{\rm sel}$ do not. Three rungs follow, and they
are the design space:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{clll}
\toprule
rung & structure & parameters reaching the field & per-proposal cost \\
\midrule
0 & conditional counts, frozen $W$ & none & $\sim0$ \\
1 & conditional counts, live $\pi_{pg}$ & $\Omega_m,w_0,w_a,\delta,\theta_{\rm sel}$ (\emph{not} $H_0$) & $\sim1$\,ms \\
2 & unconditional counts & the above, plus $H_0$ through $n_0(c/H_0)^3$ & rung 1 $+$ $\log\det H$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent Rung 1 is the target. It satisfies ``every cosmological proposal changes
the galaxy-field likelihood consistently'' for every parameter the galaxy field can
honestly constrain, and it is affordable. Rung 0 is its control arm and its
fallback: it is exactly rung 1 evaluated at $\Delta\theta=0$.

\subsection{The \texorpdfstring{$n_0$--$H_0$}{n0-H0} degeneracy: what rung 2 actually costs}
\label{sec:n0h0}

Un-conditioning restores the shell-total factor, and the restored channel is
\begin{equation}
  T_g(\theta)\;\propto\;n_0\,(c/H_0)^3\int_g C_{\rm sel}(z;\theta_{\rm sel})\,(1+z)^{\delta}\,
  \text{shape}(z;\Omega_m,w_0,w_a)\,dz ,
  \label{eq:Tg}
\end{equation}
so the \emph{entire} $H_0$ content of the count channel is the volume--density
amplitude $n_0H_0^{-3}$, exactly degenerate with $n_0$. It is identified only if
$n_0$ carries an $H_0$-independent prior --- and the shipped calibration does not:
it computes $n_0$ by dividing the summed galaxy weights by a comoving volume
integral evaluated at the \emph{fiducial} cosmology. Pinning $\log_{10}n_0$ and then
sampling $H_0$ against an unconditional count likelihood therefore manufactures a
standard-density $H_0$ constraint out of the fiducial cosmology, acting against
$22.79$M galaxies. The honest sentence, which belongs verbatim in any paper:

\begin{quote}\itshape
The galaxy field constrains the shape parameters and the tracer biases. It
constrains $H_0$ only through the volume--density amplitude, which is degenerate
with the mean comoving number density; that degeneracy is why $n_0$ is a nuisance
and why its prior must be $H_0$-aware rather than a fiducial plug-in.
\end{quote}

\subsection{Marginalising the field: the galaxy-side evidence}
\label{sec:laplace}

\begin{proposition}[the galaxy-side evidence]
\label{prop:laplace}
With the whitened prior,
\begin{equation}
  \log\mathcal L_{\rm gal}(\theta)
  =\log\!\int\!d\xi\,\mathcal N(\xi;0,\mathbb 1_M)\,p(\{N\}|\xi,\theta)
  \;\simeq\;-J_\theta(\hat\xi_\theta)-\tfrac12\log\det H(\theta),
  \label{eq:laplace}
\end{equation}
with \emph{no} residual $(2\pi)^{\pm M/2}$: the prior normalisation and the Gaussian
integral cancel exactly. Equivalently, with
$\ell(\xi,\theta)=\sum_g\sum_pN_{pg}\log\pi_{pg}$,
\begin{equation}
  \log\mathcal L_{\rm gal}(\theta)
  =\ell(\hat\xi_\theta,\theta)\;-\;\tfrac12\big\|\hat\xi_\theta\big\|^2
  \;-\;\tfrac12\log\det H(\theta).
  \label{eq:evidence}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\noindent The middle term of \eqref{eq:evidence} is an Occam factor and it is
\emph{not} removable by the envelope theorem: stationarity gives
$\partial_\xi\ell(\hat\xi)=\hat\xi$, not $0$, so the envelope theorem removes
$\partial\hat\xi/\partial\theta$ from $J$ and not from $\ell$. Its size is not
academic: with $\sim10^3$ data-constrained modes, $\|\hat\xi\|\sim32$, and a
displacement of the mode of order $\|\Delta\hat\xi\|_H\sim6$ across the prior gives
$\hat\xi^{\!\top}S\,\Delta\theta$ of order a few nats at random alignment and up to
$O(10^2)$ nats worst case, against a target accuracy of $0.1$ nat. Under linear
response it is also free:
\begin{equation}
  \tfrac12\|\hat\xi_\theta\|^2
  =\tfrac12\|\hat\xi_{\rm ref}\|^2
  +\big(S^{\!\top}\hat\xi_{\rm ref}\big)\!\cdot\!\Delta\theta
  +\tfrac12\Delta\theta^{\!\top}\big(S^{\!\top}S\big)\Delta\theta ,
\end{equation}
one precomputed vector and one small matrix. Likewise $\log\det H$ is not
\emph{assumed} constant: its motion enters multiplied by $M/2\simeq1.9\times10^3$,
so a $0.1\%$ per-mode drift is $1.9$ nats, and it is measured directly at the same
solves that produce $S$ --- the Cholesky factor is already formed.

\subsection{Linear response: the \texorpdfstring{$\theta$}{theta}-coupled ensemble}
\label{sec:linresp}

\paragraph{Offline, once.} From the implicit function theorem, against the same
Cholesky factor,
\begin{equation}
  S=\frac{\partial\hat\xi}{\partial\theta}
   =-H^{-1}\frac{\partial\,\nabla_\xi J}{\partial\theta}\bigg|_{\hat\xi_{\rm ref},\theta_{\rm ref}}
   \;\in\;\mathbb R^{M\times n_\theta},
  \label{eq:S}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ $n_\theta$ extra triangular solves and $121$\,kB of storage at $M=3780$,
$n_\theta=4$. At $K\ge2$ each catalog's own selection parameters and its own bias
contribute their own \emph{columns} of $S$: the multitracer generalisation adds
terms, it does not restructure anything.

\paragraph{Per proposal.} At fixed $H$, a mean shift is exact:
\begin{equation}
  \xi_m(\theta)=\hat\xi_{\rm ref}+S\,\Delta\theta+L_H^{-\!\top}g_m
  \label{eq:meanshift}
\end{equation}
is an \emph{exact} draw from $\mathcal N(\hat\xi_\theta,H^{-1})$ for every $\theta$,
with $g_m$ fixed across proposals. There are no importance weights and no
effective-sample-size penalty. This is worth stating sharply because the obvious
alternative is a trap:

\begin{proposition}[the effective-sample-size law for a reused draw set]
\label{prop:esslaw}
If instead one keeps the draws at $\hat\xi_{\rm ref}$ and re-weights, then for a
Gaussian mode shift at fixed $H$,
$\Var[\log w]=\|\hat\xi_\theta-\hat\xi_{\rm ref}\|_H^2$ exactly, so
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\ESS(\theta)}{\Mdraw}\simeq
  \exp\!\Big(-\big\|\hat\xi_\theta-\hat\xi_{\rm ref}\big\|_H^2\Big).
  \label{eq:esslaw}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\noindent The norm is a \emph{sum over all $M$ modes}. A tolerance written per mode
as $\|\Delta\hat\xi\|_H/\sqrt M<\tau$ implies $\ESS/M\simeq e^{-\tau^2M}$: at
$M=3780$ and a comfortable-looking $\tau=0.1$ this is $4\times10^{-17}$. Reusing a
fixed draw set across proposals is inadmissible; the mean-shifted construction
\eqref{eq:meanshift} is the one to build.

\paragraph{What linear response costs.} The correction $S\Delta\theta$ is
\emph{member-independent}, and that single fact carries the whole cost model. The
member row factors stay static under a $256$-fold evaluation concurrency; the only
per-proposal additions are one member-independent row-factor contraction
($\sim1$\,ms against a $27.5$--$49.3$\,ms baseline, and a $1.46$\,MB transient that
must be reserved rather than assumed) and a rank-$n_\theta$ update of two sky
moments. It is also \emph{analytically smooth} in $\theta$: no solver, no
tolerance, no iteration count, no history --- so the discontinuity failure mode of a
per-proposal re-solve cannot occur by construction.

\paragraph{Why not simply re-solve.} Because of arithmetic, not taste. The separable
two-stage contraction costs $2G_s\Npix\Msph^2$ for the diagonal part plus
$2\Msph^2G_s\Mz^2$ for the radial contraction, and the Cholesky costs $M^3/3$; at
$(\Msph,\Mz,G_s)=(315,12,32)$ that is tens of milliseconds per Newton step and
$\sim13$ steps, i.e.\ $\sim1$\,s per proposal against a $27.5$\,ms baseline. A
re-solve would also drag the member row factors into the per-evaluation transient,
$3.0$\,GB at $\Mdraw=8$ and $24$\,GB at $\Mdraw=64$ at production concurrency. The
asymmetry, not elegance, is why Proposition~\ref{prop:cancel} is the load-bearing
result.

\subsection{The budget normaliser in closed form}

The normaliser that enforces the gauge of Proposition~\ref{prop:gauge} against the
\emph{consumption} weights is
\begin{equation}
  \rho_m(z;\theta,b)
  =\log\frac{\sum_{p\in\mathcal F}\big[1-f_pC(z;\theta)\big]e^{\,bf_m(p,z)}}
            {\sum_{p\in\mathcal F}\big[1-f_pC(z;\theta)\big]}
  =\log\frac{A_m(z;b)-c\,B_m(z;b)}{P_{\mathcal F}-c\,F_{\mathcal F}},
  \qquad c\equiv C(z;\theta),
  \label{eq:rho}
\end{equation}
with $A_m=\sum_{p\in\mathcal F}e^{bf_m}$, $B_m=\sum_{p\in\mathcal F}f_pe^{bf_m}$,
$P_{\mathcal F}=|\mathcal F|$, $F_{\mathcal F}=\sum_{p\in\mathcal F}f_p$. The sums run
over the \emph{fitted footprint}: off the footprint $f_p=0$, the consumption weight
is unity and $Q\equiv1$, so that block satisfies the budget identity trivially and
must not absorb the footprint's monopole. $A_m$ and $B_m$ are $\theta$-free and are
computed once on a grid in $b$; the only online $\theta$-dependence is the scalar
$c$ at each node, so \eqref{eq:rho} is exact rather than interpolated. This is what
removes a full-sky reduction --- measured at $25.6$\,ms in double precision, i.e.\
$+50$--$90\%$ of the entire likelihood --- from the per-proposal path. Under linear
response the moments acquire $\theta$-dependence only through the same projection,
$\partial A_m/\partial\theta_j=\sum_pe^{bf_m^{\rm ref}}(\Phi_p\!\cdot\!S_j)$, at
$1.1$\,MB of extra storage.

The identity the design must preserve, exactly, per realisation, is
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\sum_{p=1}^{\Npix}\big[1-f_pC(z;\theta)\big]\,Q_p(z)}
       {\sum_{p=1}^{\Npix}\big[1-f_pC(z;\theta)\big]}\;=\;1
  \qquad\text{for every }z,\text{ member and }\theta .
  \label{eq:budget}
\end{equation}

\subsection{The member estimator, predicted before it is measured}
\label{sec:est}

The largest open number in the design is the member spread $\sigma$ of
Proposition~\ref{prop:jensen}, because $\Mdraw$ depends on it exponentially. It can
be predicted in closed form from objects that already exist. To first order in the
field, the gradient of the total event log-likelihood with respect to the latent is
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;a\;\equiv\;\nabla_\xi\Big(\sum_i\ell_i\Big)
   \;=\;\bgw\Big(\sum_i\varphi_i\,\Phi_i-N_{\rm obs}\langle\Phi\rangle_{\rm sel}\Big),
   \qquad
   \sigma=\big\|L_H^{-1}a\big\|_2 \;}
  \label{eq:sigma}
\end{equation}
where $\varphi_i$ is event $i$'s fraction of prior mass in the missing branch and
$\langle\Phi\rangle_{\rm sel}$ is the selection-weighted basis average. The norm is
the $H^{-1}$ norm, not the Euclidean one: for members drawn as in
\eqref{eq:laplacedraws}, $\Var_m(\ell_m)=a^{\!\top}H^{-1}a$ exactly. The subtraction
term is the detection normalisation, and it is what makes \eqref{eq:sigma} a
correlation statistic rather than a raw pair count.

The same object $a$ does three jobs, which is the reason to build it first. It
predicts $\sigma$ and hence $\Mdraw$. It gives the closed-form target for the
small-bias limit of the estimator: expanding \eqref{eq:membermarg} to second order,
\begin{equation}
  \LSE_m\ell_m-\log\Mdraw-\ell(\hat\xi)\;\longrightarrow\;\tfrac12\,a^{\!\top}H^{-1}a
  \;=\;\tfrac12\sigma^2 ,
  \label{eq:P17}
\end{equation}
so prediction and validation become one measurement, checked against truth rather
than against a larger draw count. (With the counts switched off, $\hat\xi=0$ and
$H=\mathbb 1$, and \eqref{eq:P17} reduces to the pure prior form
$\tfrac12\bgw^2\sum_{ij}K(x_i,x_j)$ --- the two-arm structure is what makes this a
genuine closed-form test rather than a certificate of prior collapse.) And it
supplies the criterion of the next subsection.

\subsection{Two numbers decide the whole design}
\label{sec:criterion}

What decides whether the field must be coupled to the proposal is not whether the
fitted field \emph{looks} different --- it is whether the \emph{likelihood} is
different, in nats. To first order $\Delta\log\mathcal L=a\cdot\Delta\hat\xi$, and
Cauchy--Schwarz gives $|\Delta\log\mathcal L|\le\sigma\,\|\Delta\hat\xi\|_H$. A
per-mode tolerance is therefore indeterminate by $\sqrt M\simeq61.5$ depending on an
alignment between the field displacement (a radial re-weighting of shells) and the
event gradient (the localisation volumes) that nothing bounds and that has every
reason to be non-negligible, since both live in the same radial direction. Do not
bound the alignment; measure it. The two criteria, both in nats, both computable
from the same set of solves at prior-drawn $\theta$:
\begin{align}
  \osc_\theta\,\Delta\log\mathcal L_{\rm GW}
  &=\osc_\theta\Big[\,a\cdot\big(\hat\xi_\theta-\hat\xi_{\rm ref}\big)\Big],
  \label{eq:critGW}\\[2pt]
  \osc_\theta\,\Delta\log\mathcal L_{\rm gal}
  &=\osc_\theta\Big[\,\ell(\hat\xi_\theta,\theta)-\tfrac12\|\hat\xi_\theta\|^2
      -\tfrac12\log\det H(\theta)\Big].
  \label{eq:critgal}
\end{align}

\begin{remark}[they are not the same number]
\label{rem:notsame}
It is tempting to argue that if the field barely moves then the galaxy-side
evidence cannot be informative either. That is false, and the direction of the
error matters. The evidence responds to the \emph{full} count residual
$r=N-T\pi$ contracted with $\partial_\theta\eta$; the field displacement is that
residual \emph{projected onto} $\mathrm{span}(\Phi)$ and damped by $H^{-1}$. A
$\theta$-direction lying inside $\mathrm{span}(\Phi)$ is absorbed by the field ---
large displacement, flat evidence, no constraint. A direction orthogonal to it
cannot be absorbed --- zero displacement, maximal evidence constraint. Since
\eqref{eq:cancelresidual} makes the $\theta$-direction exactly a \emph{within-shell}
covariance, and a shell-collapsed basis cannot represent within-shell structure,
``small displacement with a live galaxy-side constraint'' is the structurally
expected case, not an excluded one. \eqref{eq:critGW} and \eqref{eq:critgal} must
both be measured and reported.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[and the galaxy-side channel must not be quoted as cosmology]
\label{rem:effectA}
$\Delta\log\mathcal L_{\rm gal}$ acts against $22.79$M galaxies while the
gravitational-wave side carries $259$ events, so it can dominate the posterior. But
it is a $dN/dz$-shape constraint, exactly degenerate with the radial evolution index
$\delta$, with the population-average photometric-redshift kernel and with the shell
binning --- all of which are frozen inside $W$ and none of which are sampled. It is
therefore reported as a diagnostic and does not enter a headline posterior unless
$W$'s own parameters are sampled or profiled. Otherwise the headline result is a
galaxy-clustering measurement wearing a dark-siren hat.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Three limits, one hierarchy}
\label{sec:limits}

Write the single-event likelihood as
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal L_i(\Lambda,\theta,\xi)
  =\int\!dz\,d\bn\;p_i\big(d_i\,\big|\,d_L(z;\Lambda),\bn\big)\,
  \frac{\Lcat(\bn,z)+\Lmiss(\bn,z;\xi)}{\int[\Lcat+\Lmiss]} .
  \label{eq:master}
\end{equation}
All three established analyses are limits of \eqref{eq:master}, and marginalising
the field is exactly the operation that interpolates between them.

\paragraph{Limit I --- complete catalog, good localisation.} $f_pC\to1$ gives
$\Lmiss\to0$, and at $\bgw=\bgal$ the catalogued branch of \eqref{eq:zpriornew} is
field-free, so
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal L_i(\Lambda)\;\longrightarrow\;
  \sum_{j\in\Omega_{90}^{(i)}}w_j(\Lambda)\,p_i\big(d_i\,\big|\,d_L(z_j;\Lambda),\bn_j\big),
  \label{eq:limit1}
\end{equation}
the classical weighted-host dark siren, with $\xi$ dropping out of
\eqref{eq:master} \emph{identically}, member by member. \textbf{Exact.} This is a
physics identity, not a routing property, and it is the strongest inertness
statement the design admits --- but only at $\bgw=\bgal$; otherwise the excess-bias
factor of \eqref{eq:zpriornew} survives and the limit is deliberately not an
identity.

\paragraph{Limit II --- incomplete, good localisation.} Both terms live. Because a
well-localised event spans $O(1$--$10)$ correlation cells at the smoothing scale,
the likelihood depends on the field essentially through its point value in the
localisation volume, and
\begin{equation}
  \log\mathbb E_\xi[\mathcal L_i]\simeq\log\bar{\mathcal L}_i
  +\bgw\langle s\rangle_i
  +\tfrac12\bgw^2\Big[\Var_\xi\langle s\rangle_i-\langle\sigma^2\rangle_i\Big]+O(b^3).
  \label{eq:limit2}
\end{equation}
The first-order term is the placement \emph{information}; the second-order term is
the placement \emph{uncertainty}, and it is exactly what the member ensemble
estimates. This is the regime every real catalog/detector pairing occupies.

\paragraph{Limit III --- diffuse localisation: a correlation-function statement.}
Let the catalogued fraction of each event's prior mass tend to zero while the
localisation spans many correlation cells. The galaxy counts are \emph{retained} ---
they are what carries $\bgal$. Linearising both the event factor and the count factor
in the field and marginalising the Gaussian latent jointly,
\begin{equation}
  \log\!\int\!d\xi\,\mathcal N(\xi;0,\mathbb 1)\,e^{(a_{\rm GW}+a_{\rm gal})\cdot\xi}
  =\tfrac12\|a_{\rm GW}\|^2+a_{\rm GW}\!\cdot\!a_{\rm gal}+\tfrac12\|a_{\rm gal}\|^2 ,
  \label{eq:limit3}
\end{equation}
with $a_{\rm GW}=\bgw\sum_i\Phi_i$ and
$a_{\rm gal}=\bgal\sum_{p,g}(N_{pg}-T_g\pi_{pg})\Phi_{pg}$. \emph{Marginalising the
Gaussian latent replaces the field by its kernel}, and every pair of field-coupled
objects contributes $b_ab_bK(x_a,x_b)$: event$\,\times\,$event is the
gravitational-wave auto-correlation; event$\,\times\,$galaxy-voxel is
\begin{equation}
  \bgw\bgal\sum_i\sum_{p,g}(N_{pg}-T_g\pi_{pg})\,K(x_i,x_{pg})
  \;\longrightarrow\;
  \bgw\bgal\sum_\ell\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}\,\mathcal W^i_\ell\,\mathcal W^g_\ell\,
  C_\ell^{\rm GW\times g},
  \label{eq:limit3harmonic}
\end{equation}
the harmonic cross-correlation estimator, with the per-event localisation windows
$\mathcal W^i_\ell$ playing the role of tomographic bin windows and the bias
combination $\bgw\bgal$ being exactly what such a measurement constrains; and
voxel$\,\times\,$voxel is the galaxy auto-correlation, i.e.\ the count likelihood
itself. \textbf{Approximate:} \eqref{eq:limit3}--\eqref{eq:limit3harmonic} are the
$O(b^2)$ truncation of \eqref{eq:master}, valid in the Gaussian, weakly clustered
regime, and the expansion parameter $\bgw s$ over the events' support is a measured
quantity, not an assumed one.

\paragraph{The unification statement, and its honest scope.} Limits I--III are not
three methods; they are one likelihood at three completeness/localisation regimes,
and the field-level hierarchy is the interpolant that is valid at both ends and,
unlike either, in the middle. Two scope statements ride with this. Limit III is a
property of \emph{the hierarchy}; the practical estimator conditions on the galaxy
data first, absorbing the counts into $\hat\xi$ and $H$, so the cross term is not a
separate object of the estimator. And at rung 0 that conditioning happens at frozen
$\theta$, which removes precisely the cosmology dependence of
$C_\ell^{\rm GW\times g}$ that a cross-correlation measurement uses --- which is an
argument \emph{for} the $\theta$-coupled rung, not against the unification.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Offline anchor construction (once per catalog set)}
\label{alg:offline}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE catalogs $\{k\}$, areal-completeness maps $\{f_k\}$, anchor
         $(\theta_{\rm ref},\Lambda_{\rm ref})$, bias grid $\{b\}$
\STATE \textbf{basis:} inducing nodes $\Msph\times\Mz$; $L\leftarrow\mathrm{chol}(K_{ZZ}+j\mathbb 1)$;
       form $\Phi_{\rm sph}$ and $\Phi_\zeta$ \COMMENT{never a dense $\Phi$: $47.6$\,GB at $M=3780$}
\STATE \textbf{counts:} $N_{kpg}\leftarrow\mathrm{histogram}(z_{\rm gal};\text{shell edges})$
       \COMMENT{integer, exact; edges stamped, never inherited from a global $z_{\max}$}
\STATE \textbf{shell response:} $W_{gn}$ by \eqref{eq:Wdef}, rows normalised;
       $\tilde\phi_\zeta\leftarrow W\phi_\zeta$
       \COMMENT{photo-$z$ convolved \emph{forward}, into the basis}
\STATE \textbf{solve:} $\hat\xi,H\leftarrow$ Fisher scoring on $J_{\theta_{\rm ref}}$ with
       \eqref{eq:multgrad}--\eqref{eq:multhess} \COMMENT{separable two-stage contraction}
\STATE \textbf{sensitivities:} $S\leftarrow-H^{-1}\partial_\theta\nabla_\xi J$ by
       \eqref{eq:S}; also $\partial\hat\xi/\partial b$
       \COMMENT{$n_\theta{+}1$ triangular solves against the same $L_H$; $121$\,kB}
\STATE \textbf{Occam pre-contraction:} store $S^{\!\top}\hat\xi_{\rm ref}$ and $S^{\!\top}S$
       \COMMENT{makes \eqref{eq:evidence} free online}
\STATE \textbf{draws:} $\xi_m=\hat\xi+L_H^{-\!\top}g_m$, $g_m$ in antithetic pairs, seed stamped
\STATE \textbf{moments:} $A_m,B_m$ on the $b$-grid over $\mathcal F$, and their
       projections $\partial A_m/\partial\theta$, $\partial B_m/\partial\theta$
\STATE \textbf{stamp:} content hash over basis, kernel, jitter, $N_{\rm side}$, $f_p$,
       $W$, shell edges, counts, $\theta_{\rm ref}$, $\{b_k\}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Per-proposal likelihood, rung 1 (linear response)}
\label{alg:online}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE $(H_0,\Lambda,\{\theta_k\},\{b_k\},\bgw)$; anchor artefact of Alg.~\ref{alg:offline}
\STATE $\Delta\theta\leftarrow\theta-\theta_{\rm ref}$
       \COMMENT{$H_0$ is absent by Prop.~\ref{prop:cancel}}
\STATE $\Delta\Xi\leftarrow\mathrm{reshape}(S\,\Delta\theta,(\Msph,\Mz))$
       \COMMENT{member-independent: this is the whole cost model}
\STATE $\log\mathcal L_{\rm gal}\leftarrow
        \ell_{\rm ref}+\partial_\theta\ell\cdot\Delta\theta
        -\tfrac12\|\hat\xi_{\rm ref}\|^2-(S^{\!\top}\hat\xi_{\rm ref})\!\cdot\!\Delta\theta
        -\tfrac12\Delta\theta^{\!\top}(S^{\!\top}S)\Delta\theta
        -\tfrac12\log\det H$
\STATE $c(z)\leftarrow C(z;\theta)$ \COMMENT{a scalar per redshift node}
\STATE $A\leftarrow A^{\rm ref}+(\partial A/\partial\theta)\Delta\theta$; likewise $B$;
       $\rho_m\leftarrow\log[(A_m-cB_m)/(P_{\mathcal F}-cF_{\mathcal F})]$ by \eqref{eq:rho}
\FOR{each member $m$}
  \STATE $\log Q_m(p,z)\leftarrow
         \bgw\big[(\mathrm{row\_fac}_m[p]+\Delta\Xi[p])\cdot\tilde\phi_\zeta[z]\big]-\rho_m(z)$
         \COMMENT{$0$ exactly for $p\notin\mathcal F$ and for $z>\zdep$}
  \STATE $\ell_m\leftarrow\textsc{EventTerms}(\log Q_m)-N_{\rm ev}\log\mu(\log Q_m)$
         \COMMENT{same realisation in both, enforced}
\ENDFOR
\STATE $\log\mathcal L\leftarrow\log\mathcal L_{\rm gal}+\LSE_m\ell_m-\log\Mdraw$
\STATE report member $\ESS=\exp(-\sum_mp_m\log p_m)$, $p_m=\mathrm{softmax}_m(\ell_m)$,
       and the two channels of \eqref{eq:critGW}--\eqref{eq:critgal} separately
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Cost}

The baseline is measured, not modelled: $27.5$\,ms per likelihood call in a single
pass and $49.3$\,ms under a poorly chosen block plan, at production dimensions, with
$256$ concurrent evaluations and $10.4$\,GiB of $72.7$\,GiB already static. The
production configuration carries \emph{no} structure at all, so the honest added cost
of the deliverable is the whole member-marginalised slice, not the difference against
a table-marginalised run:

\noindent Three structural facts hold this together. The compression from a member
\emph{table} cube to member \emph{row factors} is a factor $N_{\rm grid}/\Mz\simeq90$
($1.71$\,GB $\to$ $11.7$\,MB at $\Mdraw=8$), which is what makes a defensible
$\Mdraw$ affordable at all under \eqref{eq:Mrequired}. The full-sky budget reduction
leaves the per-proposal path entirely, in closed form, by \eqref{eq:rho}. And the
$\theta$-correction is member-independent, so the resident latent arrays stay static
($+140$ to $+225$\,MB) and the only per-evaluation transient is the $1.46$\,MB row
expansion of $S\Delta\theta$, i.e.\ $\sim375$\,MB at $256$-fold concurrency ---
reserved explicitly, not assumed. Under any per-proposal re-solve variant every one
of those three facts fails simultaneously: the moments regain $\theta$-dependence
($+50$--$90\%$), the row factors become transient ($3$--$24$\,GB), and the evaluation
goes memory-bound before it goes compute-bound.

\subsection{When to stop}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.1em,style=nextline,itemsep=1pt]
\item[K0 (day 3).] If the event-weighted in-support prior mass is below $10^{-3}$
\emph{and} switching the existing table on at the anchor moves the log-likelihood by
less than $0.1$ nat across the $H_0$ prior, the field cannot move the posterior. Stop.
The deliverable is the bounded systematic, and it is three days away rather than
thirty-six. (Context: the in-support missing budget is $\sim2\times10^{-5}$ of the total by
three independent integrals; the production $H_0$ posterior is already identical with
and without a table; and most event posterior samples lie above $\zdep$.)
\item[K2.] If neither latent-on versus latent-off nor $\theta$-coupled versus frozen
moves the $H_0$ median by $0.1\sigma$ or the $90\%$ interval width by $5\%$, on
closure \emph{and} on a non-railing production configuration, stop at that rung and
publish the bound as a systematic.
\item[K3.] Fitted-versus-truth slope below $0.8$ at the guard-compliant rank: the
basis is collapsing (the pathological value is $0.04$).
\item[K4.] Added wall beyond the accepted budget with the accuracy target unmet:
descend one rung at a time --- $\theta$-coupled $\to$ frozen ensemble $\to$
fixed-realisation systematic --- rather than jumping to the weakest fallback.
\item[K5.] Member effective sample size below $2$ of $\Mdraw$ with the
$\theta$-varying bias unmeetable at $\Mdraw\le128$: do not ship it as a
marginalisation.
\item[K8.] The areal-completeness change is \emph{expected} to be large --- the
$18\,682$ empty pixels alone move the all-sky missing budget by $\sim+45\%$ --- so if
it moves the $H_0$ posterior by more than $1\sigma$ with the field off, that change
is a result in its own right and ships as its own arm; the field programme continues
behind it.
\item[K9 (day 14).] If \eqref{eq:critGW} exceeds $0.1$ nat and the linear-response
residual cannot be brought below $0.1$ nat with a second-order term and multiple
anchors, then a re-solve is required and a re-solve is not affordable: ship the
frozen ensemble and quote the measured $\theta$-dependence as a systematic.
Symmetrically, if \eqref{eq:critGW} is below $0.1$ nat the coupling adds nothing on
the gravitational-wave side --- but by Remark~\ref{rem:notsame} that says nothing
about \eqref{eq:critgal}, which is gated separately.
\item[K10.] If the unconditional rung is taken and $n_0$ is not sampled under an
$H_0$-aware prior (or reparameterised $H_0$-invariantly), and likewise for
$\theta_{\rm sel}$ against its anchored magnitude prior, any $H_0$ shift is an
artefact of the fiducial calibration. Refuse the arm rather than publish it.
\end{description}

\subsection{What not to attempt}

A full per-proposal Newton re-solve ($\sim36\times$ the entire likelihood, and it
makes the member arrays transient). Reusing one draw set across proposals with
importance weights ($\ESS/M=4\times10^{-17}$ at a per-mode displacement that reads as
comfortably tight). Adding the count likelihood while leaving the field a plug-in
$\hat\xi(\mathcal C)$ --- Remark~\ref{rem:doublebreak}(i). Materialising $\Phi$
($47.6$\,GB at $M=3780$). Sampling $\xi$ in the outer chain (the production run has
$20$ free dimensions; $M\ge2520$ is not a perturbation on that). Widening shells past
the photometric-redshift scatter. And building the multitracer table producer: the
field-level path makes it unnecessary, and it would inherit that producer's missing
budget gauge and its $1.34$\,Gpc rank floor. One caution on rank: a feasibility
argument made at $M\sim10^2$ is made at a rank the resolution and isotropy guards
forbid --- feasibility here comes from the correction being member-independent and of
rank $n_\theta$, not from a small $M$.

\section{Decisions required}
\label{sec:decisions}
% =====================================================================

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.0em,itemsep=3pt]

\item[\bf D1.] \textbf{Which rung?}
\emph{(a)} conditional counts, frozen response: no $\theta$ coupling; the control arm
and the fallback.
\emph{(b)} conditional counts, linear response: coupling for
$\Omega_m,w_0,w_a,\delta,\theta_{\rm sel}$, no $H_0$ --- \textbf{recommended},
conditional on the day-14 criterion.
\emph{(c)} unconditional counts: adds $H_0$ only through $n_0(c/H_0)^3$, admissible
only with D6 resolved and \S\ref{sec:n0h0} stated in the paper.
\emph{(d)} unconditional counts with a per-proposal re-solve: infeasible.
\emph{Trade:} conditioning gives up the $dN/dz$ constraint on the budget, restored
explicitly as a prior rather than as a by-product of the field.

\item[\bf D2.] \textbf{Jitter convention} for the Kronecker basis. \emph{Recommend}
an absolute, amplitude-independent $10^{-6}$ on both factors, stamped. \emph{Cost:}
latent-mode $Q$ differs from any legacy table by $\sim2\times10^{-3}$ at the guard
rank, so every migration comparison references a same-convention rebuild.

\item[\bf D3.] \textbf{Smoothing scale.} \emph{Recommend} the isotropic
$\sim190$\,Mpc kernel ($\ell_{\rm ang}=0.2$, $\ell_\zeta=0.039$, $\Msph=315$,
$\Mz=8$--$12$). \emph{Cost:} the analysis is explicitly a $190$\,Mpc-smoothed density
field. \emph{Alternative:} keep $50$\,Mpc radially and accept a $4{:}1$ anisotropic
kernel measuring $20\%$ of its own radial signal --- which must then be stated as
such.

\item[\bf D4.] \textbf{Per-pixel areal completeness.} \emph{Recommend} $f_p$ on
\emph{both} sides with $C_p=f_pC(z)$ in the missing budget --- exact for areal
masking. \emph{Fallback:} restrict the count channel to a mask-homogeneous
sub-footprint, exact without touching the completeness side, losing $\sim30\%$ of
area. Not an option: ignoring it, which inverts the sign of the inferred
incompleteness.

\item[\bf D5.] \textbf{Marginalisation-accuracy budget, hence $\Mdraw$.}
\emph{Recommend} $0.1$ nat of $\theta$-varying bias across the $H_0$ prior, with
$\Mdraw$ set against \eqref{eq:Mrequired} and $\sigma$ measured in the $H^{-1}$ norm
of \eqref{eq:sigma}. \emph{Cost, against the true baseline:} $+31\%$ at $\Mdraw=8$,
$+125\%$ at $32$, $+250\%$ at $64$. \emph{Alternative:} the fixed-realisation
systematic --- zero cost, reduced claim. Note this arithmetic holds only under linear
response.

\item[\bf D6.] \textbf{Budget uncertainty.} \emph{Recommend} sampling
$(\log_{10}n_0,\delta)$ under the calibration covariance, systematics-inflated,
rather than the current plug-in from the same counts. \textbf{Required, not
recommended, under D1(c)}: the calibration must be re-derived as a function of $H_0$,
or $n_0$ reparameterised to an $H_0$-invariant combination such as $n_0h^{-3}$.

\item[\bf D7.] \textbf{Above the survey depth.} \emph{Recommend} $Q\equiv1$ with zero
variance in the headline analysis, with the amplitude profile delivered as a
sensitivity scan quoted as ``$H_0$ shift under an assumed amplitude'', never as a
marginalised posterior --- because no measurement in this programme constrains the
field there.

\item[\bf D8.] \textbf{Is the gravitational-wave bias free, and what does that imply
for the catalogued branch?} \emph{Recommend} $\bgw\equiv\bgal$ in the headline run:
it makes Limit I exact and the complete-catalog test a genuine physics identity, at
zero cost. Wherever $\bgw$ is free --- the mock campaign, the multitracer tier --- the
excess-bias factor $e^{(\bgw-\bgal)s(x_j)}$ \textbf{must} be written on the catalogued
spike weights. Writing that branch field-free is a modelling error, and it is the
first thing a cross-correlation-literate referee checks.

\item[\bf D9.] \textbf{Tracer overlap at $K\ge2$.} \emph{Recommend} a disjoint
partition. The event-side mixture is safe under overlap; the count-side product is
not.

\item[\bf D10.] \textbf{Field family for the headline run, and whether to keep the
table path.} The production table is radial, and the radial builder is a per-pixel
quasi-Newton loop that cannot move inside the likelihood at any rung --- so adopting
the latent path \emph{is} a change of family, by necessity rather than preference.
\emph{Recommend} running four arms of one comparison before choosing --- radial table
/ low-rank table / frozen latent / $\theta$-coupled latent --- and keeping the table
path indefinitely as the golden-pinned reference. Note that the $K\ge2$
parametric-selection \emph{table} arm cannot be built today at any $K$
(\S\ref{sec:seam}).

\item[\bf D11.] \textbf{The novelty search.} Resolved by scheduling: it is the first
item of work, not the last. No ``first'' appears anywhere until it is written down.

\item[\bf D12.] \textbf{What is the headline?} Strip what is already scored as not
novel and the budget-preserving marginalisation reduces to a gauge fixing
(Prop.~\ref{prop:gauge}) plus a correctness requirement (same realisation in the event
terms and the normalisation, already enforced); with the mandatory scope caveats what
remains is \emph{``we marginalise over the angular placement of missing hosts inside
the surveyed volume at fixed budget.''} \emph{Recommend (a):} headline the
\textbf{unification} --- Limits I--III as one likelihood, with the $\theta$-coupled
field as the object that makes the interpolation real --- and demote the
budget-preserving marginalisation to the enabling clause it is. \emph{(b)} headline
the marginalisation and accept that it may read as a methods note. \emph{(c)} defer
until the day-3 search returns.

\item[\bf D13.] \textbf{Does the galaxy-side evidence enter the headline posterior?}
It acts against $22.79$M galaxies while the events number $259$, and it is a
$dN/dz$-shape constraint degenerate with $\delta$, with the photometric-redshift
kernel and with the shell binning --- all frozen inside $W$. \emph{Recommend (a):}
report it as a diagnostic; it does not enter the headline posterior unless $W$'s own
parameters are sampled or profiled. \emph{(b)} admit it and sample/profile those
parameters --- real work, and the only honest route to quoting cosmology from it.
\emph{(c)} admit it with $W$ frozen: refused.

\item[\bf D14.] \textbf{Is the magnitude channel a likelihood factor or an anchored
prior?} Today it is an anchored Gaussian prior at $1.6\times10^{-4}$\,mag; the
magnitude log-likelihood exists but is never evaluated. \emph{Recommend (a):} keep
empirical Bayes, state it plainly, and let the rung-dependent guards carry the
consequences --- zero work, honest. \emph{(b)} implement it as a likelihood factor and
drop the anchored prior: this closes \eqref{eq:hierarchy} exactly and is what would
permit the unconditional rung without double-counting $\theta_{\rm sel}$, at the cost
of real work.

\end{enumerate}

\vspace{0.6em}
\noindent\textbf{The two decisions that gate everything else are D1 and D12}, and
both are answerable within three days of starting: D1 by the day-3 measurement of
whether the field can move the posterior at all, and D12 by the paper-by-paper search
that the same three days contain.
